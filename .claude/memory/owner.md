# The owner

How the owner of this library works, as learned from sessions in this repository and its siblings. Update when a session shows something new.

- Writes short, fast messages, often from a phone; typos are normal ("grench" was French). Read for intent, and ask only when two readings lead to different work.
- Does not want to check or click anything: every finished piece of work goes all the way to merged on `master` in the same session (see `CLAUDE.md`). The one thing only they can do is a repository setting, such as turning on Pages; ask for exactly that, with the URL.
- Reads Polish and German, and learned school subjects under Polish names. A Polish comparison (ą, ę, ń, the Polish „u") helps on a sounds page, and they asked for Polish and German inventories beside English and French on the second day: compare with all four where a sound is in question. **No `## Po polsku, w skrócie` or `## Auf Deutsch: Stichwörter` here**: on 2026-10-07 they had both sections removed from every page of this library (the sibling libraries keep them). Polish stays only as content, below the English in a lesson table.
- Is learning French with Assimil's *French with Ease*; the audio is on their external drive, unreachable from a cloud session.
- Asks "is it correct" about dictionary conventions (Oxford's IPA) and values a checked answer over one from memory: when a session runs on the iMac through Remote Control it can read the dictionary pages; a cloud session cannot, and says so.
- Keeps three sibling libraries (math, learning to learn, Rust) built the same way; a page here links across rather than repeating them.
- Has a French teacher, Yosser, whose worksheets go into `02_Yosser_teacher`, one lesson each, and a Polish textbook by Szypowska whose lessons go into `03_Szypowska`; "keep adding stuff as lessons" is the standing instruction for both. Sends the pages as photos, a few at a time, mid-session, with a one-line instruction ("now lesson 3 and 4"); transcribe each batch and expect the next.
- **Wants no introductions on a lesson page**: the pages are for their own learning. No Level, One line, "what the lesson is" or "how to read the table" (said on 2026-10-07, on seeing lesson 4 of Szypowska). A lesson page is: title, the rules of the lesson in a few bullets, the table, an AI section, See also. **No program and no output block either** ("no need for any python", 2026-10-07, on seeing lesson 4 of Szypowska): the IPA is checked by a CI tool instead. Instead of the program they asked for creativity: an **AI section** at the end of each lesson, "new ideas and exercises and new explanations", "new words in new contexts", and (2026-10-07) each word the lesson introduces in two or three set phrases or combinations, to help memorising; context for particular words and expressions, always in the French / English (Polish below) / IPA table. The reading conventions live on the chapter page, and a lesson links to them. **Every French word or sentence on a page carries its IPA, the rules bullets included** (asked 2026-10-07 with lesson 6), and everything of mine stays in the separate AI section, nothing of mine above it. Extra material at the bottom of a lesson is welcome (grammar notes, more examples, related words), in the same French / English (Polish below) / IPA table.
- Uses Anki. Asked (2026-10-07) for a deck of the Szypowska book with all the words, questions, phrases, exercises and challenges of my own: generated from each lesson's table plus a hand-written challenge deck per lesson, combined into one file per chapter, as the math library does for sets. Wants the decks as four sets, one file each, never mixed (2026-10-07): Szypowska (FR-PL), Szypowska (PL-FR) with the book's Polish on the front, Yosser (FR-EN), Yosser (EN-FR). In the French-front sets the IPA goes under the French on the front, not on the back (asked 2026-10-07). A new lesson's cards are handed over as that lesson's two import files, not the whole sets (asked 2026-10-07 with lesson 6); `tools/combine_anki.py` writes them. Exercises sit in the French-front set, the hand-written challenges under the to-French set. **The sound-drill rows never become cards** (rule given 2026-10-07, after lesson 6 produced cards such as *bien, chien* → “ien to /jɛ̃/”): a row that pairs letters with a sound is not a word with a meaning, so `tools/anki_from_tables.py` skips the section “The sounds drilled …”; the rows stay in the lesson table as the book's content.
- Wants such material as one table per lesson, French | English (Polish below it, in the same cell) | IPA, one line per sentence, headings as bold rows, Le Robert's key, ‿ for liaison, an answer to a blank in square brackets. Polish explanations are not transcribed; a Polish exercise that asks for a translation gets the French and IPA with English and Polish below. Was told the teacher's material would be public on the site and chose the library anyway (2026-10-07).
- Sends YouTube pronunciation Shorts (Parlez-vous French, Anne) with “I want the words and IPA”, and said where they go: a chapter called Pronunciation, `04_Pronunciation`, in French, English and IPA, no Polish (2026-10-09). The video's words cannot be read from the cloud or the iMac; when asked, the owner sends a screenshot of the screen (the words with the letters in red), and that is the source of the list. Resends the same link by mistake sometimes (“one more” with the first URL): compare the video id before making a page.
- Learns vocabulary with an app of a thousand common words and sends its entries as screenshots, one word each, several in a sitting (2026-10-09): “create a folder 1000 words and add these entries, add IPA, and at the bottom your own examples with IPA”, then “create separate pages for these words”, with grammar explanations and helpful phrases. They go in `05_1000_words`, one page per word: the app's entry first, with IPA on every line, everything of mine under the AI heading (grammar in bullets and a conjugation table, set phrases, my examples, exercises); the chapter page numbers the words in order of arrival. **No intro on a word page** (“remove the intro - who cares”, 2026-10-09, on seeing the source bullet of *dormir*): no bullet on where the entry comes from, no “mine, not the app's” lead-in under the AI heading; the page opens with the title, the Conventions line and the bullets about the word itself, and the source note lives on the chapter page. More words will come the same way; a word already on a page is not added again, and the owner resends one by mistake sometimes (*devoir* and *aller* came twice on the first day), so compare each screenshot with the chapter's index before making a page, and say in the reply which were skipped.
- After sending worksheet screenshots, says “add to 1000 words : all the words from screenshots” (2026-10-10, sections 7 to 10 of lesson 1, 27 pages): every noun, adjective, verb, preposition and connective of the sections becomes a word page, in order of appearance; articles, numbers, pronouns and the instruction verbs do not, and the reply says which words were left out and why.
- Sends words for `05_1000_words` as a plain list too, “Add to 1000 words” and lines of *word — translation* (2026-10-10), or one word alone (“add page to 1000 words : une chaise”), without the app's screenshot: make the pages in the order sent, with the list's translation as the entry and an example after the app's pattern, and say on the chapter page which pages wait for their screenshot.
- Sends a screenshot of a worksheet section and asks “do we have this text already?” or “do we have a lesson?”, then wants a reading page with the IPA after each sentence even when the text is on the lesson page (2026-10-10, twice): the lesson's table is for study, the reading page for reading aloud on the phone; build the second from the first, and when the section is already on the lesson page, say where and add it to the reading page in the same reply.
- Asks which AI model is best for quick, accurate answers on French (2026-10-09): answer in chat with a dated verdict and how sure, and keep the dated paragraph on the resources page current when the line-up changes.
- Pastes a note from another AI assistant with the one word “French:” in front (2026-10-10, on *la lie, le lit, il lit*): it means put this in the library. Check every claim, give the verdict in chat, extend the page that already has the word, and keep the how-sure table on a lesson rather than on a word page. Reminded in the same session, mid-work, that every French word gets its IPA after it: the standing rule above, which covers prose, bullets and tables alike.
- Sends a link to a shared Google Sheet with “add to french resources” (2026-10-10, Refold's *French Database*): a row in the table of `RESOURCES.md` and a section with a how-sure line, not a page.
- Asks which books with audio are best for self-study beside the teacher (2026-10-10, from the math library’s session): a verdict in chat, kept as the books section of `RESOURCES.md`, by job (one course, one drill book, readers, Polish-base drills), each with a how-sure line. A book verdict goes there from now on; the phonetics books stay on the pronunciation page.
