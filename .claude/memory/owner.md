# The owner

How the owner of this library works, as learned from sessions in this repository and its siblings. Update when a session shows something new.

- Writes short, fast messages, often from a phone; typos are normal ("grench" was French). Read for intent, and ask only when two readings lead to different work.
- Does not want to check or click anything: every finished piece of work goes all the way to merged on `master` in the same session (see `CLAUDE.md`). The one thing only they can do is a repository setting, such as turning on Pages; ask for exactly that, with the URL.
- Reads Polish and German, and learned school subjects under Polish names. A Polish comparison (ą, ę, ń, the Polish „u") helps on a sounds page, and they asked for Polish and German inventories beside English and French on the second day: compare with all four where a sound is in question. **No `## Po polsku, w skrócie` or `## Auf Deutsch: Stichwörter` here**: on 2026-10-07 they had both sections removed from every page of this library (the sibling libraries keep them). Polish stays only as content, below the English in a lesson table.
- Is learning French with Assimil's *French with Ease*; the audio is on their external drive, unreachable from a cloud session.
- Asks "is it correct" about dictionary conventions (Oxford's IPA) and values a checked answer over one from memory: when a session runs on the iMac through Remote Control it can read the dictionary pages; a cloud session cannot, and says so.
- Keeps three sibling libraries (math, learning to learn, Rust) built the same way; a page here links across rather than repeating them.
- Has a French teacher, Yosser, whose worksheets go into `02_Yosser_teacher`, one lesson each, and a Polish textbook by Szypowska whose lessons go into `03_Szypowska`; "keep adding stuff as lessons" is the standing instruction for both. Sends the pages as photos, a few at a time, mid-session, with a one-line instruction ("now lesson 3 and 4"); transcribe each batch and expect the next.
- **Wants no introductions on a lesson page**: the pages are for their own learning. No Level, One line, "what the lesson is" or "how to read the table" (said on 2026-10-07, on seeing lesson 4 of Szypowska). A lesson page is: title, the rules of the lesson in a few bullets, the table, the program's output with three short lines on it, a short Po polsku and Auf Deutsch, See also. The reading conventions live on the chapter page, and a lesson links to them. Extra material at the bottom of a lesson is welcome (grammar notes, more examples, related words), in the same French / English (Polish below) / IPA table.
- Uses Anki. Asked (2026-10-07) for a deck of the Szypowska book with all the words, questions, phrases, exercises and challenges of my own: generated from each lesson's table plus a hand-written challenge deck per lesson, combined into one file per chapter, as the math library does for sets. Wants the Yosser decks separate from the Szypowska ones (2026-10-07): one combined file per chapter, never one across chapters.
- Wants such material as one table per lesson, French | English (Polish below it, in the same cell) | IPA, one line per sentence, headings as bold rows, Le Robert's key, ‿ for liaison, an answer to a blank in square brackets. Polish explanations are not transcribed; a Polish exercise that asks for a translation gets the French and IPA with English and Polish below. Was told the teacher's material would be public on the site and chose the library anyway (2026-10-07).
