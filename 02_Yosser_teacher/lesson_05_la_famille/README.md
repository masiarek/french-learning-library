- **Son, sa, ses** /sɔ̃, sa, se/ is *his* and *her* both, and agrees with the thing owned, never with the owner: *Nadia et sa mère* /nadja e sa mɛʁ/ Nadia and her mother, *Sami et sa mère* /sami e sa mɛʁ/ Sami and his mother, the same *sa*; *Nadia et son père* /nadja e sɔ̃ pɛʁ/ Nadia and her father. English picks *his* or *her* by the owner; French picks *son* or *sa* by the noun, as Polish *swój, swoja* does. The whole set, *mon* to *leurs* /mɔ̃, lœʁ/, is on the page of [une mère](../../05_1000_words/mere/README.md).
- **Le fils** /lə fis/: the *l* is silent and the *s* is said, in the plural too, *mes fils* /me fis/ my sons. The same letters are also the plural of *le fil* /lə fil/ the thread: *les fils* /le fil/, the wires, with the *l* said and the *s* silent.
- **Une petite fille** /yn pətit fij/ is a little girl; **une petite-fille**, with the hyphen and the same sound, is a granddaughter, and *un petit-fils* /œ̃ pəti fis/ a grandson. *La fille* /la fij/ is a daughter after a possessive, *ma fille* /ma fij/, and a girl after an article, *une fille* /yn fij/.
- **Mon ami** and **mon amie** sound the same, /mɔ̃n‿ami/: the liaison gives the /n/, and the final *e* is silent. Only the spelling says a man or a woman; aloud, the name does: *mon amie Lina* /mɔ̃n‿ami lina/.
- **No article before a job after être**: *mon père est médecin* /mɔ̃ pɛʁ ɛ medsɛ̃/, *ma mère est enseignante* /ma mɛʁ ɛ(t)‿ɑ̃sɛɲɑ̃t/, where English says *a doctor, a teacher*. With *c’est* the article comes back: *c’est un médecin* /sɛ(t)‿œ̃ medsɛ̃/. *Médecin* has two syllables, /medsɛ̃/: the middle *e* is silent.
- **Gentil** /ʒɑ̃ti/, **gentille** /ʒɑ̃tij/: the masculine’s *l* is silent, the feminine’s *ill* is /j/, as in *fille* /fij/ and *famille* /famij/. The exceptions that keep the /l/, *ville* /vil/, *mille* /mil/, *tranquille* /tʁɑ̃kil/, are on the page of [une famille](../../05_1000_words/famille/README.md).
- **Je n’ai pas d’enfants** /ʒə ne pa dɑ̃fɑ̃/: after a negation *des* /de/, *un* /œ̃/ and *une* /yn/ become *de* /də/, and *d’* before a vowel: *j’ai un frère* /ʒe œ̃ fʁɛʁ/, *je n’ai pas de frère* /ʒə ne pa də fʁɛʁ/. The rule is in [lesson 2](../lesson_02_etre_avoir/README.md).
- **Frères et sœurs** /fʁɛʁ e sœʁ/: French has no everyday word for *siblings*. *La fratrie* /la fʁatʁi/ exists, but it is the word of a form or a psychologist. *Des frères et sœurs* and *des frères et des sœurs* are both said; the slides use both.
- **À a city, en a feminine country, au a masculine one**: *à Tunis* /a tynis/, *en Tunisie* /ɑ̃ tynizi/, *en France* /ɑ̃ fʁɑ̃s/, *au Canada* /o kanada/, *aux États-Unis* /oz‿etaz‿yni/.
