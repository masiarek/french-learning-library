## AI section: new contexts, new words, new exercises

Mine, not the source's. The audio, made on the owner's Mac with the [recipe in Resources](../../RESOURCES.md#audio-of-a-lesson-on-a-mac), reads the table above and then these exercises, with a pause before each answer.

- **Hello against goodbye.** *Bonjour* /bɔ̃ʒuʁ/ and *bonsoir* /bɔ̃swaʁ/ say hello; *bonne journée* /bɔn ʒuʁne/, *bon après-midi* /bɔn‿apʁɛmidi/, *bonne soirée* /bɔn swaʁe/ and *bonne nuit* /bɔn nɥi/ wish a good stretch of time and so say goodbye. The short word greets, the long word *-ée* or *après-midi* sends off.
- **The *s* of *à plus* /a plys/ is heard**, unlike *plus* /ply/ in *à plus tard* /a ply taʁ/ or *ne … plus* /nə … ply/. Written in a text message as *A+*.
- **Bonjour comes first, always.** In a shop, a bakery, a bus, *bonjour* /bɔ̃ʒuʁ/ before any question; *bonjour, madame* /bɔ̃ʒuʁ, madam/ or *bonjour, monsieur* /bɔ̃ʒuʁ, məsjø/ is politer still. Skipping it is the real common mistake.
- **Coucou** /kuku/ is for family, close friends and children, and for a text message; *salut* /saly/ for friends and colleagues of your age; *bonjour* /bɔ̃ʒuʁ/ for everyone else.

| Claim on the card | Verdict | How sure | Why |

| *Salut* /saly/ is informal, hello and goodbye | right | high | from memory |
| *Au revoir* /o ʁəvwaʁ/ is the formal goodbye | nearly | high | it is the neutral one, fine with friends too; *salut* /saly/ is what is informal |
| “Never say *bon matin* /bɔ̃ matɛ̃/” | right for France | high | in Québec *bon matin* is a normal greeting; in France it sounds like a translation from English |
| *Bon après-midi* /bɔn‿apʁɛmidi/ only to say goodbye | right | high | like *bonne journée* /bɔn ʒuʁne/: a wish for what follows, said on leaving |
