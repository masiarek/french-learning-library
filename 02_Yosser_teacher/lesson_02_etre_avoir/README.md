## AI section: new contexts, new words, new exercises

Mine, not the teacher's. The audio of this lesson, made on the owner's Mac, reads the table above and then these exercises in the same order, with a pause before each answer; the recipe is in [Resources](../../RESOURCES.md#audio-of-a-lesson-on-a-mac).

- **A slip on the worksheet.** Exercise 2, number 1 prints *Je ________ un livre* /ʒə … œ̃ livʁ/, but the only answer that fits the book is *ai* /e/, and *je* /ʒə/ drops its *e* before a vowel: *j’ai un livre* /ʒe œ̃ livʁ/. Number 6 prints it right, *J’________ 7 ans*.
- ***Ils sont* /il sɔ̃/ and *ils ont* /ilz‿ɔ̃/ differ by one sound**, /s/ against /z/. The *s* of *ils* /il/ is silent before a consonant and sounds /z/ before a vowel, so *ils ont* is the one with the buzz. It is the first thing to listen for in the audio.
- **Age, hunger, cold and fear take *avoir* /avwaʁ/**, where English says *to be*: *j’ai treize ans* /ʒe tʁɛz ɑ̃/, *j’ai faim* /ʒe fɛ̃/, *j’ai froid* /ʒe fʁwa/, *j’ai peur* /ʒe pœʁ/. *Je suis treize ans* is a classic English speaker's mistake.
- **After *pas* /pa/, *un, une, des* /œ̃, yn, de/ become *de* /də/**: *j’ai un chat* /ʒe œ̃ ʃa/, *je n’ai pas de chat* /ʒə ne pa də ʃa/. Only after *avoir* and other verbs with an object; after *être* /ɛtʁ/ nothing changes, *ce n’est pas un chat* /sə nɛ pa(z)‿œ̃ ʃa/.
- **The *-t-* of *a-t-il* /atil/** keeps two vowels apart when the verb ends in one: *il a* /il a/ becomes *a-t-il ?* /atil/, *elle a* /ɛl a/ becomes *a-t-elle ?* /atɛl/. The same *-t-* is in the questions of [lesson 1](../lesson_01_la_maison/README.md), *quel âge a-t-il ?* /kɛl ɑʒ atil/.
