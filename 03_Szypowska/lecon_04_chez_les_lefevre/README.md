# Lesson 4: Chez les Lefèvre

## The rules of the lesson

- **The letter c** is /s/ before *i* (*ici, voici*), /k/ before *a* and *u* (*cabinet, cuisine*), and /k/ at the end of a short word after a vowel (*Bac, avec*).
- **Elision:** *le* loses its vowel before a vowel: *l'appartement*.
- **The genitive, continued.** *de* alone before a proper name (*de Jean, de Marie, de Monsieur Lefèvre*); *du*, contracted from *de* + *le*, before a masculine noun beginning with a consonant (*du garçon*); *de la* before a feminine noun beginning with a consonant (*de la famille*); *de l'* before any noun, of either gender, beginning with a vowel or a mute *h* (*de l'amie*); *des*, contracted from *de* + *les*, before a plural noun (*des parents*).
- **Realia.** The rue du Bac, literally Ferry Street, runs towards the Seine, to the place where a ferry once crossed and a bridge now stands.

## AI section: new contexts, new words, new exercises

Mine, not the book's. Notes first, then each word of the lesson in set phrases, the lesson's words in new sentences, a few new words, and two exercises with the answers.

- *Chez* + a person is "at that person's place": *chez les Lefèvre, chez moi, chez le médecin*. No single English word does this.
- *Habiter* takes the place directly or with *à* or *dans*: *habiter Paris, habiter à Paris, habiter un appartement*. *Vivre* is "to live" in the sense of being alive.
- *Visiter* is for places (*visiter un appartement, visiter Varsovie*). For a person French says *rendre visite à*: a classic trap.
- *La fille* is both daughter and girl; the son is *le fils* /fis/, with the *s* sounded.
- *Entrer dans*, *sortir de*: the preposition goes with the verb, *entrer dans la cuisine, sortir de la cuisine*.
- *Du* in this lesson is only *de + le*. It is also "some", *du pain*, which comes later.
