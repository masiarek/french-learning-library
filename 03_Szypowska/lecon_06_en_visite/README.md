# Lesson 6: En visite

## The rules of the lesson

- **The dative.** *À* before a proper name: *à Toutou* /a tutu/, *à Mme Lefèvre* /a madam ləfɛvʁ/. *Au* is *à* + *le*, before a masculine noun beginning with a consonant: *au garçon* /o ɡaʁsɔ̃/. *À la* before a feminine noun beginning with a consonant: *à la fille* /a la fij/. *À l'* before any noun beginning with a vowel or a mute *h*: *à l'enfant* /a lɑ̃fɑ̃/. *Aux* is *à* + *les*, before a plural noun: *aux enfants* /oz‿ɑ̃fɑ̃/.
- **Adjectives.** The feminine adds *-e*: *un petit jardin* /œ̃ pəti ʒaʁdɛ̃/, *une petite maison* /yn pətit mɛzɔ̃/. The plural adds *-s*: *la grande personne* /la ɡʁɑ̃d pɛʁsɔn/, *les grandes personnes* /le ɡʁɑ̃d pɛʁsɔn/. An adjective agrees in gender and number with its noun, as in Polish.
- **Plurals.** A noun in *-eau* takes *-x*: *un gâteau, des gâteaux* /œ̃ ɡɑto, de ɡɑto/; *l'oiseau, les oiseaux* /lwazo, lez‿wazo/. *Monsieur, messieurs* /məsjø, mesjø/ and *madame, mesdames* /madam, medam/ are compounds in which both halves change: *mon sieur, mes sieurs; ma dame, mes dames*.
- **The definite article** for things already known (*les amis passent bien la journée* /lez‿ami pas bjɛ̃ la ʒuʁne/, *les deux enfants* /le døz‿ɑ̃fɑ̃/) and for a kind in general: *j'aime les arbres et les fleurs* /ʒɛm lez‿aʁbʁ e le flœʁ/, *les oiseaux chantent dans les arbres* /lez‿wazo ʃɑ̃t dɑ̃ lez‿aʁbʁ/. No article in the vocative: *Chers amis !* /ʃɛʁz‿ami/.
- **Inversion after direct speech**, in the inserted clause: *dit-il* /ditil/, *dit Mme Lefèvre* /di madam ləfɛvʁ/, *demande-t-elle* /dəmɑ̃dtɛl/.

## AI section: new contexts, new words, new exercises

Mine, not the book's: everything below this heading is added, and nothing above it is. Notes first, then each word of the lesson in set phrases, the lesson's words in new sentences, a few new words, and two exercises with the answers.

- *Monsieur* is /məsjø/: the *on* is a schwa and the *r* is silent; the plural *messieurs* is /mesjø/. *Madame* /madam/, *mesdames* /medam/.
- *Neuf* is /nœf/, but /nœv/ in liaison before *ans* and *heures*: *neuf ans* /nœv‿ɑ̃/, *neuf heures* /nœv‿œʁ/. *Six* and *dix* are /sis, dis/ alone, /si, di/ before a consonant (*six livres* /si livʁ/), /siz, diz/ in liaison (*six ans* /siz‿ɑ̃/). *Huit* drops its *t* before a consonant: *huit jours* /ɥi ʒuʁ/.
- *Gâteau* is /ɡɑto/ in Le Robert's key, with the back /ɑ/ of *â*; most speakers today say /ɡato/, which is what the book's [gato] shows.
- *Le goûter* /lə ɡute/ is the children's afternoon snack, around four o'clock; the verb *goûter* /ɡute/ is to taste.
- *Les grandes personnes* /le ɡʁɑ̃d pɛʁsɔn/ is the children's word for adults; the neutral word is *les adultes* /lez‿adylt/.
- *Toutou* /tutu/ is a child's word for a dog, like "doggy", used here as the dog's name. *Chaville* /ʃavil/ is a real town west of Paris, between Sèvres and Versailles.
- *Donner* takes the thing directly and the person with *à*: *donner un livre au garçon* /dɔne œ̃ livʁ o ɡaʁsɔ̃/. So do *offrir, apporter, dire, demander*.
