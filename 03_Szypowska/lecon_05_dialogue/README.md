# Lesson 5: Dialogue

**Level:** 101 · for a Polish reader working through the fifth lesson of Szypowska's course

**One line:** The first page of lesson 5, page 21 of the book, as one table: the sounds drilled at the top and the dialogue, eleven lines about having and not having a pen, a notebook, a penknife, pencils, with *est-ce que*, the inverted question, and *ne … pas de*. A program types in the book's bracketed transcriptions and holds them to the page's IPA: eleven brackets, ten letter for letter, and one where the book prints [że] for *je*. The rest of the lesson is not in hand yet; this page grows when it arrives.

## What the page is

The lesson opens with a sound drill, four sounds and the letters that spell them: *c* as /k/ before *a*, *o* and a consonant (*cahier, canif, carnet, crayon*), *-ay-* as /ɛj/ (*crayon*), *-ai, -ez, -es* as /e/ (*j'ai, avez, des*) and the unaccented *e* of *que, de* as /ə/. Then the dialogue, each line with the book's transcription under it and, in the book, a curved line under *vous avez* and *nous avons* to mark the liaison.

## How to read the table

- **French** is the text as printed; bold rows are the sections.
- **English, with the Polish below it.** Both are mine: the page has no word list yet. *Stylo* is a fountain pen and *plume* the nib, or a pen with one; the Polish keeps *pióro* for both.
- **IPA** follows Le Robert's key, written from memory. ‿ marks a compulsory liaison, the two the book underlines: *vous‿avez* /vuz‿ave/, *nous‿avons* /nuz‿avɔ̃/.

## What the dialogue shows

- **Two ways to ask.** *Est-ce que j'ai un stylo ?* puts *est-ce que* before the statement; *Avez-vous un carnet ?*, *As-tu un canif ?*, *A-t-il une plume ?* invert verb and pronoun, with a *-t-* slipped in where two vowels would meet.
- **Not having.** After a negation the article *un, une, des* becomes *de*: *je n'ai pas de canif*, *nous n'avons pas de cahiers*.
- **The liaison in the plural pronouns.** *vous‿avez, nous‿avons*: the silent *s* of *vous, nous* sounds as /z/ before the vowel.

What the output settles:

1. **Ten of the eleven lines agree letter for letter**, the two liaisons included: the book's [wuzawe] and [nuzawõ] are the page's /vuz‿ave/ and /nuz‿avɔ̃/ once the mark is set aside.
2. **One line parts, and the program points at the symbol.** For *je n'ai pas de canif* the book prints [że ne pa …], which the key turns into /ʒe/, where *je* is /ʒə/; the page keeps /ʒə/. Either the book misprints the schwa, which it writes correctly as [lə] and [də] everywhere else, or the scan is read wrong; the printed page would settle it.
3. **The page is clean**: every symbol is a French phoneme, with no stress mark and no length mark.

## Po polsku, w skrócie

Pierwsza strona piątej lekcji w jednej tabeli: ćwiczenie wymowy (c jako /k/, -ay- jako /ɛj/, -ai, -ez, -es jako /e/, e bez akcentu jako /ə/) i dialog o tym, kto ma pióro, notes, scyzoryk, ołówki, a kto nie ma. Dialog pokazuje dwa sposoby pytania („est-ce que" i inwersję, z wstawionym „-t-" w „a-t-il"), przeczenie „ne … pas de" zamiast „un, une, des" i łączenie międzywyrazowe w „vous‿avez", „nous‿avons". Angielski i polski są moje, bo słówek na tej stronie jeszcze nie ma. Program przepisuje 11 nawiasów z książki i porównuje je ze stroną: 10 zgadza się co do znaku, a w jednym książka drukuje [że] zamiast [żə] dla „je". Reszta lekcji dojdzie, gdy właściciel prześle kolejne strony.
