# Les sons [ɑ̃] et [ɔ̃] — français, anglais, IPA

## The rules

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- The source is Anne’s Short *Pratiquons ensemble les sons [ã] et [ɔ̃]* /pʁatikɔ̃ ɑ̃sɑ̃bl le sɔ̃ ɑ̃ e ɔ̃/ on the channel Parlez-vous FRENCH (48 seconds, [youtube.com/shorts/owz6E6qa2ig ↗](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/owz6E6qa2ig)). She writes the first sound [ã]; Le Robert writes the same sound /ɑ̃/, and so does every page here.
- /ɑ̃/ is spelled *an, am, en, em* when a consonant follows or the word ends there: *blanc* /blɑ̃/, *la chambre* /la ʃɑ̃bʁ/, *l’enfant* /lɑ̃fɑ̃/, *le temps* /lə tɑ̃/. The *m* spellings come before *b* and *p*.
- /ɔ̃/ is spelled *on, om* in the same positions: *bon* /bɔ̃/, *le nom* /lə nɔ̃/, *l’ombre* /lɔ̃bʁ/, *le pompier* /lə pɔ̃pje/.
- The nasal goes away when a vowel or a doubled *n* or *m* follows: *bon* /bɔ̃/ but *bonne* /bɔn/, *un an* /œ̃n‿ɑ̃/ but *une année* /yn ane/, *Simon* /simɔ̃/ but *Simone* /simɔn/. In liaison *bon* is read like *bonne*: *un bon ami* /œ̃ bɔn‿ami/.
- Where the spelling lies: *en* is /ɛ̃/ in *l’examen* /lɛɡzamɛ̃/ and *l’agenda* /laʒɛ̃da/; *emm-, enn-* at the start of a word keep the nasal, *emmener* /ɑ̃mne/, *l’ennui* /lɑ̃nɥi/, but *l’ennemi* /lɛnmi/ does not; *la femme* /la fam/, *évidemment* /evidamɑ̃/, *l’automne* /lotɔn/ and *monsieur* /məsjø/ have no nasal at all.
- How to make them: for /ɑ̃/ open the mouth as for *a* and let the air out through the nose too, as in *dans* /dɑ̃/; for /ɔ̃/ round the lips as for *o* and do the same, as in *dont* /dɔ̃/. For a Polish ear, *ą* in *wąs* is close to /ɔ̃/; /ɑ̃/ has no Polish letter and is the one to practise. For an English ear neither exists: the vowel of *bond* with the lips rounded is near /ɔ̃/, the vowel of *father* with the nose open near /ɑ̃/. These comparisons are written from memory and are approximate.
- The spelling rules are those of the channel’s own pages on the letters *on, om* and *en, em* (parlez-vous-french.com), read from search summaries because the site is blocked from the cloud; the examples below are mine and the transcriptions follow Le Robert’s key from memory. The video’s own list of words could not be read: YouTube and its caption service are blocked from the cloud, and the iMac session reached the title and the description but not the captions. The video’s words go at the top of the table as soon as a screenshot of them arrives.

## AI section: new contexts, new words, new exercises

Mine, not the video’s. Notes first, then the key words of the drill in set phrases, the two sounds in new sentences, new words that carry them, and two exercises with the answers.

- *Son* is three words with one sound: *son livre* (his book), *le son* (the sound) and *le son* (bran). *Ton* is *your* and also *le ton*, the tone.
- *Dont* is the relative pronoun for *de*: *le livre dont je parle* /lə livʁ dɔ̃ ʒə paʁl/, the book I am talking about.
- *Vont* is the third person plural of *aller*: *ils vont* /il vɔ̃/. Said alone it is the whole difference from *le vent*.
- *Un bon vin blanc* /œ̃ bɔ̃ vɛ̃ blɑ̃/ is the classic phrase with all four nasal vowels; *un grand pain rond* /œ̃ ɡʁɑ̃ pɛ̃ ʁɔ̃/ has three. Both are in the table above and in the challenge deck.
- *Cent ans* takes a liaison, /sɑ̃t‿ɑ̃/; *onze ans* does not, /ɔ̃z ɑ̃/: the *z* of *onze* is its own letter, said in any case, and *onze* never takes a liaison before it either, *les onze* /le ɔ̃z/.
