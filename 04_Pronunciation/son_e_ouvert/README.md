# Le son [ɛ] — français, anglais, IPA

## The rules

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- The source is a screenshot the owner sent from a Short of the same series by Anne on Parlez-vous FRENCH: five words down the screen, the letters that make the sound in red. The sound is /ɛ/, the open *e* of *elle* /ɛl/; the video’s title and link were not sent, so the page names the words and not the video.
- The five spellings the video picks out: *est* /ɛ/, the whole word; *ai* in *mais* /mɛ/; *è* in *lève* /lɛv/; *ê* in *être* /ɛtʁ/; and a plain *e* before a doubled consonant in *appelle* /apɛl/.
- The rule behind them: *e* is /ɛ/ when its syllable ends in a consonant that is said, *elle* /ɛl/, *le sel* /lə sɛl/, *avec* /avɛk/, *la lettre* /la lɛtʁ/, and /ə/ or silent when it is not, *le* /lə/, *appeler* /aple/. A doubled consonant or a grave accent is how French writes the open vowel before a consonant that would otherwise open the syllable: *j’appelle* /ʒapɛl/ but *nous appelons* /nuz‿aplɔ̃/, *je lève* /ʒə lɛv/ but *nous levons* /nu ləvɔ̃/.
- *è* and *ê* are always /ɛ/: *le père* /lə pɛʁ/, *très* /tʁɛ/, *la fête* /la fɛt/, *même* /mɛm/. *ai* and *ei* are /ɛ/ too: *le lait* /lə lɛ/, *faire* /fɛʁ/, *la neige* /la nɛʒ/, *seize* /sɛz/, except *ai* at the end of a verb in the first person, *j’ai* /ʒe/, *je parlai* /ʒə paʁle/, which Le Robert gives as /e/.
- The pair that matters most: *et* /e/ against *est* /ɛ/, *mes* /me/ against *mais* /mɛ/, *les* /le/ against *le lait* /lə lɛ/. Le Robert keeps /e/ and /ɛ/ apart at the end of a word; many speakers, in the south above all, say /e/ for both, so a learner who hears no difference is hearing real French. Said from memory, with the dictionary’s side given on every row below.
- For a Polish ear, /ɛ/ is the Polish *e* of *ten*; the new sound is /e/, closer, as in *été* /ete/, which Polish does not have. For an English ear, /ɛ/ is the vowel of *bed* and /e/ the first half of *day* without the glide. Both comparisons are approximate.

## AI section: new contexts, new words, new exercises

Mine, not the video’s. Notes first, then the video’s five words in set phrases, the sound in new sentences, new words that carry it, and two exercises with the answers.

- *Est* is the verb and *et* the conjunction: *il est grand et blond* /il ɛ ɡʁɑ̃ e blɔ̃/. The ear tells them apart by the vowel alone, which is why the video starts with *est*.
- *Mais* /mɛ/ and *mes* /me/ are a true minimal pair in Le Robert; so are *le lait* and *les*. A learner who says /me/ for both is understood everywhere.
- The doubled consonant is a spelling device, not a long sound: *appelle* /apɛl/ has one /l/, as *belle* /bɛl/ and *elle* /ɛl/ do.
- *Lève* and *appelle* are the *je, tu, il* forms, and also the imperative: *Lève la main !* /lɛv la mɛ̃/, *Appelle-moi !* /apɛl mwa/.
- *Être* /ɛtʁ/ ends in a consonant cluster with no vowel after it; the *e* at the end is silent, as in *lettre* /lɛtʁ/ and *quatre* /katʁ/.
