# confortable — comfortable

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *confortable* /kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/: *con-* is the nasal /ɔ̃/ of *bon* /bɔ̃/ and *mon* /mɔ̃/, in [Les sons ɑ̃ et ɔ̃](../../04_Pronunciation/sons_an_on/README.md); *-fort-* is /fɔʁ/, the word *fort* /fɔʁ/ strong; and *-able* is /abl/, as in *la table* /la tabl/ and *agréable* /aɡʁeabl/, the final *-e* silent and the *bl* said. The spelling has an *n* where English *comfortable* has an *m*: *confortable*, *le confort* /lə kɔ̃fɔʁ/.
- One form for both genders: an adjective that already ends in *-e* takes no feminine *-e*, so *un lit confortable* /œ̃ li kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/ and *une chaise confortable* /yn ʃɛz kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/ are spelt and said alike; the plural adds a silent *-s*, *des lits confortables* /de li kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/. Four uses, one sound. *Agréable, pratique, moderne, calme, rouge, jeune* /aɡʁeabl, pʁatik, mɔdɛʁn, kalm, ʁuʒ, ʒœn/ behave the same way.
- It goes **after** the noun, as most adjectives do: *une maison confortable* /yn mɛzɔ̃ kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/, *un fauteuil confortable* /œ̃ fotœj kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/. The short adjectives *grand, petit, beau, joli* /ɡʁɑ̃, pəti, bo, ʒɔli/ go before, so the teacher's sentence puts one on each side: *une grande maison confortable* /yn ɡʁɑ̃d mɛzɔ̃ kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/, and so does Adam's *Ma maison est belle et confortable* /ma mɛzɔ̃ ɛ bɛl e kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/ in [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md).
- A false friend in one direction: a chair, a bed, a house, a car, a salary can be *confortable*, but a person cannot. Someone who is comfortable is *bien* /bjɛ̃/, *à l'aise* /a lɛz/ or *bien installé* /bjɛ̃n‿ɛ̃stale/: *je suis bien ici* /ʒə sɥi bjɛ̃ isi/ I am comfortable here, *mettez-vous à l'aise* /metevu a lɛz/ make yourself comfortable. *Je suis confortable* is heard in Quebec and marked as an anglicism by the dictionaries, from memory.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **Agreement by number only.** *Confortable* /kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/ for the masculine and the feminine, *confortables* /kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/ for both plurals, all four said the same. The written *-s* matters: *Les chaises sont confortables* /le ʃɛz sɔ̃ kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/ needs it, *La chaise est confortable* /la ʃɛz ɛ kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/ does not. The ear hears nothing; the page does.
- **After the noun, and des stays des.** *Une maison confortable* /yn mɛzɔ̃ kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/, *des maisons confortables* /de mɛzɔ̃ kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/: because the adjective follows its noun, *des* keeps its full form. It is only before a preceding plural adjective that careful French writes *de*: *de grandes maisons* /də ɡʁɑ̃d mɛzɔ̃/, *de belles chambres* /də bɛl ʃɑ̃bʁ/. With one adjective on each side, *de grandes maisons confortables* /də ɡʁɑ̃d mɛzɔ̃ kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/. The pages of [grand](../grand/README.md) and [beau](../beau/README.md) have the other half of the rule.
- **What can be comfortable.** Things you sit or lie on, *un fauteuil, un canapé, un lit, un matelas* /œ̃ fotœj, œ̃ kanape, œ̃ li, œ̃ matla/; things you wear, *des chaussures confortables* /de ʃosyʁ kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/; places and vehicles, *un hôtel, une chambre, une voiture* /œ̃n‿otɛl, yn ʃɑ̃bʁ, yn vwatyʁ/; and, as in English, money and margins: *un salaire confortable* /œ̃ salɛʁ kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/, *une avance confortable* /yn avɑ̃s kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/ a comfortable lead, *une majorité confortable* /yn maʒɔʁite kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/.
- **The person: bien, à l'aise.** *Je suis bien* /ʒə sɥi bjɛ̃/, *je suis bien installé* /ʒə sɥi bjɛ̃n‿ɛ̃stale/, *je suis à l'aise* /ʒə sɥi(z)‿a lɛz/ I am comfortable; *mal à l'aise* /mal a lɛz/ ill at ease; *Vous êtes bien installés ?* /vuz‿ɛt bjɛ̃n‿ɛ̃stale/ Are you comfortable? The adverb is fine with a person, *installez-vous confortablement* /ɛ̃stalevu kɔ̃fɔʁtabləmɑ̃/ make yourself comfortable. And the English *to comfort* someone is another word again, *réconforter* /ʁekɔ̃fɔʁte/, with its noun *le réconfort* /lə ʁekɔ̃fɔʁ/.
- **Comparing.** *Plus confortable que* /ply kɔ̃fɔʁtabl kə/, *moins confortable que* /mwɛ̃ kɔ̃fɔʁtabl kə/, *aussi confortable que* /osi kɔ̃fɔʁtabl kə/; *le plus confortable, la plus confortable* /lə ply kɔ̃fɔʁtabl, la ply kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/. Since the adjective follows the noun, the superlative repeats the article: *l'hôtel le plus confortable de la ville* /lotɛl lə ply kɔ̃fɔʁtabl də la vil/, against *la plus grande ville* /la ply ɡʁɑ̃d vil/ with one article.
- **The family.** *Le confort* /lə kɔ̃fɔʁ/ comfort, *tout confort* /tu kɔ̃fɔʁ/ with every convenience, of a flat or a hotel; *confortablement* /kɔ̃fɔʁtabləmɑ̃/ comfortably; *inconfortable* /ɛ̃kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/ uncomfortable, *l'inconfort* /lɛ̃kɔ̃fɔʁ/ discomfort. Near it: *douillet, douillette* /dujɛ, dujɛt/ cosy, *spacieux, spacieuse* /spasjø, spasjøz/ spacious, *pratique* /pʁatik/ practical, *moelleux, moelleuse* /mwalø, mwaløz/ soft, of a cushion; and the opposite in the hand, *dur, dure* /dyʁ/ hard.
