# une salle de bains — a bathroom

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *une salle de bains* /yn sal də bɛ̃/: three words, one noun. *Salle* /sal/ has a silent *-e*, *de* /də/ keeps its schwa, and *bains* /bɛ̃/ is the nasal /ɛ̃/ of *le pain* /lə pɛ̃/, *la main* /la mɛ̃/ and *demain* /dəmɛ̃/, with a silent *-s*. Szypowska's [lesson 4](../../03_Szypowska/lecon_04_chez_les_lefevre/README.md) drills the *ain* of *bain* /bɛ̃/ and writes *la salle de bain* /la sal də bɛ̃/ without the *s*; the teacher writes *la salle de bains* /la sal də bɛ̃/ with it. Both spellings are seen, and they sound the same.
- *Une* /yn/ says the noun is feminine, from *la salle* /la sal/, the room: *la salle de bains* /la sal də bɛ̃/, *ma salle de bains* /ma sal də bɛ̃/, and in the plural *deux salles de bains* /dø sal də bɛ̃/, where the *-s* goes on *salle* /sal/ and nothing changes in sound.
- The teacher's first lesson, [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md), counts them in Adam's house: *Dans ma maison, il y a un salon, une cuisine, trois chambres et deux salles de bains* /dɑ̃ ma mɛzɔ̃, il j a œ̃ salɔ̃, yn kɥizin, tʁwa ʃɑ̃bʁ e dø sal də bɛ̃/, and asks *Combien de salles de bains y a-t-il ?* /kɔ̃bjɛ̃ də sal də bɛ̃ jatil/.
- *La salle de bains* /la sal də bɛ̃/ is the room with the bath or the shower and the washbasin. In a French flat the toilet is often a room of its own, *les toilettes* /le twalɛt/, always plural, so the question for a guest is *Où sont les toilettes ?* /u sɔ̃ le twalɛt/ and not *Où est la salle de bains ?* /u ɛ la sal də bɛ̃/. A small bathroom with a shower and no bath is *une salle d'eau* /yn sal do/.
- *Une salle* /yn sal/ is a large or shared room, as Szypowska's lesson 4 says: *la salle à manger* /la sal a mɑ̃ʒe/ the dining room, *la salle de classe* /la sal də klas/ the classroom, *la salle d'attente* /la sal datɑ̃t/ the waiting room. A room in general is *une pièce* /yn pjɛs/ and a bedroom *une chambre* /yn ʃɑ̃bʁ/. *Salle* /sal/ sounds exactly like *sale* /sal/ dirty, so *une salle sale* /yn sal sal/ is a dirty room.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **Se laver, and the reflexive verbs of the bathroom.** *Se laver* /sə lave/ to wash oneself: *je me lave* /ʒə mə lav/, *tu te laves* /ty tə lav/, *il se lave* /il sə lav/, *nous nous lavons* /nu nu lavɔ̃/, *vous vous lavez* /vu vu lave/, *ils se lavent* /il sə lav/. With a part of the body French uses the article, not the possessive, because the reflexive pronoun already says whose it is: *je me lave les mains* /ʒə mə lav le mɛ̃/ I wash my hands, *elle se lave les cheveux* /ɛl sə lav le ʃəvø/ she washes her hair, *je me brosse les dents* /ʒə mə bʁɔs le dɑ̃/ I brush my teeth. The others: *se doucher* /sə duʃe/ or *prendre une douche* /pʁɑ̃dʁ yn duʃ/ to have a shower, *prendre un bain* /pʁɑ̃dʁ œ̃ bɛ̃/ to have a bath, *se raser* /sə ʁaze/ to shave, *se coiffer* /sə kwafe/ to do one's hair, *se maquiller* /sə makije/ to put on make-up, *se sécher* /sə seʃe/ to dry oneself.
- **The passé composé of se laver.** With *être* /ɛtʁ/, like every reflexive verb on the page of [être](../etre/README.md): *je me suis lavé* /ʒə mə sɥi lave/, *elle s'est lavée* /ɛl sɛ lave/, the participle agreeing with the subject; but when a part of the body follows, the agreement goes: *elle s'est lavé les mains* /ɛl sɛ lave le mɛ̃/, the same sound either way. The imperative is *lave-toi les mains !* /lav twa le mɛ̃/ wash your hands!, *lavez-vous les mains !* /lave vu le mɛ̃/.
- **In the bathroom.** *une baignoire* /yn bɛɲwaʁ/ a bath, *une douche* /yn duʃ/ a shower, *un lavabo* /œ̃ lavabo/ a washbasin, *un robinet* /œ̃ ʁɔbinɛ/ a tap, *un miroir* /œ̃ miʁwaʁ/ a mirror, *une serviette* /yn sɛʁvjɛt/ a towel, *le savon* /lə savɔ̃/ the soap, *le shampooing* /lə ʃɑ̃pwɛ̃/ the shampoo, *une brosse à dents* /yn bʁɔs a dɑ̃/ a toothbrush, *le dentifrice* /lə dɑ̃tifʁis/ the toothpaste, *un peigne* /œ̃ pɛɲ/ a comb, *un rasoir* /œ̃ ʁazwaʁ/ a razor, *un sèche-cheveux* /œ̃ sɛʃʃəvø/ a hairdryer, *l'eau chaude, l'eau froide* /lo ʃod, lo fʁwad/, *le carrelage* /lə kaʁlaʒ/ the tiles.
- **Les salles, le bain.** *La salle à manger, la salle de classe, la salle d'attente, la salle de séjour* /la sal də seʒuʁ/ the living room, *la salle de sport* /la sal də spɔʁ/ the gym, *la salle des fêtes* /la sal de fɛt/ the village hall, *une salle de cinéma* /yn sal də sinema/ a cinema screen, *une salle de concert* /yn sal də kɔ̃sɛʁ/. And *le bain* /lə bɛ̃/ on its own: *un bain de soleil* /œ̃ bɛ̃ də sɔlɛj/ sunbathing, from Szypowska's lesson 4, *un bain moussant* /œ̃ bɛ̃ musɑ̃/ a bubble bath, *un bain de foule* /œ̃ bɛ̃ də ful/ a walkabout, *un maillot de bain* /œ̃ majo də bɛ̃/ a swimsuit, *un peignoir de bain* /œ̃ pɛɲwaʁ də bɛ̃/ a bathrobe.
- **Toilettes, always plural.** *Les toilettes* /le twalɛt/, *aller aux toilettes* /ale o twalɛt/ to go to the toilet, *Les toilettes sont au fond du couloir* /le twalɛt sɔ̃t‿o fɔ̃ dy kulwaʁ/ the toilet is at the end of the corridor, *les WC* /le vese/. In the singular *la toilette* /la twalɛt/ is washing and dressing, *faire sa toilette* /fɛʁ sa twalɛt/, and a woman's outfit; *le papier toilette* /lə papje twalɛt/ is toilet paper. The sign on the door says *occupé* /ɔkype/ or *libre* /libʁ/, and so does the family: *La salle de bains est occupée !* /la sal də bɛ̃ ɛ(t)‿ɔkype/ The bathroom is taken!
