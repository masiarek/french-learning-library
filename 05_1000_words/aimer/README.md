# aimer — to like, to love

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *aimer* /eme/: *ai* is the closed /e/ when nothing follows it in the syllable, *aimer, aimé, aimez* /eme/, and the open /ɛ/ before a consonant that is said, *j'aime* /ʒɛm/, *tu aimes* /ty ɛm/, *ils aiment* /ilz‿ɛm/, the [loi de position](../../04_Pronunciation/son_e_ouvert/README.md) again. It begins with a vowel, so *je* elides, *j'aime* /ʒɛm/, and the plural pronouns make their liaison, *nous aimons* /nuz‿emɔ̃/, *vous aimez* /vuz‿eme/.
- A regular *-er* verb: *j'aime, tu aimes, il aime, nous aimons, vous aimez, ils aiment* /ʒɛm, ty ɛm, il ɛm, nuz‿emɔ̃, vuz‿eme, ilz‿ɛm/, participle *aimé* /eme/. Three spellings say /eme/, *aimer, aimé, aimez*, and four forms say /ɛm/: the lesson on [homophones](../../01_Sounds/homophones/README.md) is about exactly that.
- One verb for *like* and *love*, which is the trap. With a thing, *aimer* is to like: *j'aime le café* /ʒɛm lə kafe/ I like coffee, *j'aime beaucoup ma maison* /ʒɛm boku ma mɛzɔ̃/ I like my house a lot. With a person, *aimer* alone is to love: *je t'aime* /ʒə tɛm/ I love you; and *aimer bien* /eme bjɛ̃/ makes it weaker, *je t'aime bien* /ʒə tɛm bjɛ̃/ I like you, as a friend. So *beaucoup* and *bien* strengthen the verb with a thing and soften it with a person.
- The thing one likes takes the definite article, *le, la, les*, because one likes the thing in general: *j'aime le chocolat* /ʒɛm lə ʃɔkɔla/, *elle aime les chats* /ɛl ɛm le ʃa/, never *du chocolat* after *aimer*. A verb follows in the infinitive: *j'aime lire* /ʒɛm liʁ/ I like reading.
- The worksheet's last frame is *J'aime ma maison parce que ________.* /ʒɛm ma mɛzɔ̃ paʁs kə …/, and its model text ends *J'aime beaucoup ma maison.* /ʒɛm boku ma mɛzɔ̃/, in the teacher's [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md).

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **The scale.** With a thing: *je déteste* /ʒə detɛst/ I hate, *je n'aime pas* /ʒə nɛm pa/ I don't like, *j'aime bien* /ʒɛm bjɛ̃/ I quite like, *j'aime* /ʒɛm/ I like, *j'aime beaucoup* /ʒɛm boku/ I like a lot, *j'adore* /ʒadɔʁ/ I love. With a person the top of the scale turns over: *je t'aime bien* /ʒə tɛm bjɛ̃/ and *je t'aime beaucoup* /ʒə tɛm boku/ are friendship, *je t'aime* /ʒə tɛm/ is love, *je t'adore* /ʒə tadɔʁ/ is fond and light again. *Je l'aime* /ʒə lɛm/ I love him, I love her.
- **J'aimerais: the polite wish.** The conditional, *j'aimerais* /ʒɛməʁɛ/ I would like, is the gentlest way to ask for anything: *j'aimerais un café* /ʒɛməʁɛ œ̃ kafe/, *j'aimerais visiter Paris* /ʒɛməʁɛ vizite paʁi/, *j'aimerais bien* /ʒɛməʁɛ bjɛ̃/ I'd love to. With a different subject in the second clause the verb goes to the subjunctive: *j'aimerais que tu viennes* /ʒɛməʁɛ kə ty vjɛn/ I would like you to come, as on the [aller](../aller/README.md) page.
- **Aimer mieux, préférer, plaire.** *Aimer mieux* /eme mjø/ to prefer: *j'aime mieux le thé* /ʒɛm mjø lə te/; *préférer* /pʁefeʁe/ says the same, *je préfère le thé* /ʒə pʁefɛʁ lə te/. *Plaire* /plɛʁ/ turns the sentence round, the thing pleases the person: *ça me plaît* /sa mə plɛ/ I like it, *elle me plaît* /ɛl mə plɛ/ I fancy her, *s'il vous plaît* /sil vu plɛ/ if it pleases you, please.
- **The tenses.** Past *j'ai aimé* /ʒe eme/ I liked, I loved; imparfait *j'aimais* /ʒɛmɛ/ I used to like, I loved, the tense of feelings in the past; future *j'aimerai* /ʒɛməʁe/; conditional *j'aimerais* /ʒɛməʁɛ/, the two apart by the last vowel, /e/ and /ɛ/. Reflexive, *s'aimer* /sɛme/ to love each other: *ils s'aiment* /il sɛm/.
- **The family of the word.** *L'amour* /lamuʁ/ love, masculine, *mon amour* /mɔ̃n‿amuʁ/ my love; *amoureux, amoureuse* /amuʁø, amuʁøz/ in love, *tomber amoureux de* /tɔ̃be amuʁø də/ to fall in love with; *aimable* /ɛmabl/ kind; *bien-aimé* /bjɛ̃neme/ beloved; and the cousins *un ami, une amie* /œ̃n‿ami, yn ami/ a friend, *l'amitié* /lamitje/ friendship, from the same Latin root.
