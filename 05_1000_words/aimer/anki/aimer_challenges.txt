Say: I like my house a lot.	J’aime beaucoup ma maison. /ʒɛm boku ma mɛzɔ̃/	say
Say: I like my house because it is big.	J’aime ma maison parce qu’elle est grande. /ʒɛm ma mɛzɔ̃ paʁs kɛl ɛ ɡʁɑ̃d/	say
Say: Do you like coffee? (to a friend)	Tu aimes le café ? /ty ɛm lə kafe/	say
Say: I would like a coffee, please.	J’aimerais un café, s’il vous plaît. /ʒɛməʁɛ œ̃ kafe, sil vu plɛ/	say
Say: I would like to live in Paris.	J’aimerais habiter à Paris. /ʒɛməʁɛ abite a paʁi/	say
I love you, against I like you as a friend	je t’aime /ʒə tɛm/; je t’aime bien /ʒə tɛm bjɛ̃/: bien softens the verb with a person	grammar
Aimer /eme/ against j’aime /ʒɛm/: why two vowels?	ai is the closed /e/ at the end of the syllable and the open /ɛ/ before a consonant that is said	sound
I like chocolate: which article after aimer?	the definite one, always: j’aime le chocolat /ʒɛm lə ʃɔkɔla/, never du	grammar
I like, we like, you like (plural), they like	j’aime /ʒɛm/, nous aimons /nuz‿emɔ̃/, vous aimez /vuz‿eme/, ils aiment /ilz‿ɛm/	grammar
I will like, against I would like	j’aimerai /ʒɛməʁe/, j’aimerais /ʒɛməʁɛ/: the last vowel tells them apart	grammar
