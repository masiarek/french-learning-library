# une chaise — a chair

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *une chaise* /yn ʃɛz/: *ch* is /ʃ/, the *sh* of English *shoe*, never the /tʃ/ of English *chair*; *ai* before a consonant that is said is the open /ɛ/ of [Le son ɛ](../../04_Pronunciation/son_e_ouvert/README.md); and the final *-e* is silent but makes the *s* sound, as /z/ between vowels. So *chaise* rhymes with *fraise* /fʁɛz/, *seize* /sɛz/ and *française* /fʁɑ̃sɛz/, and is not *chez* /ʃe/, where nothing follows the vowel and it closes to /e/.
- *Une* /yn/ says the noun is feminine: *la chaise* /la ʃɛz/, *les chaises* /le ʃɛz/, *des chaises* /de ʃɛz/, where only the article changes in sound. Nouns in *-aise* are feminine, *une fraise* /yn fʁɛz/ a strawberry, *une falaise* /yn falɛz/ a cliff, *une Française* /yn fʁɑ̃sɛz/.
- The word has been on a page since the teacher's first lesson, [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md): *Dans ma chambre, il y a un lit, un bureau, une chaise et une armoire* /dɑ̃ ma ʃɑ̃bʁ, il j a œ̃ li, œ̃ byʁo, yn ʃɛz e yn aʁmwaʁ/, with the furniture and the prepositions of place.
- A chair comes with a verb learners dread, *s'asseoir* /saswaʁ/ to sit down, and with its state, *être assis* /ɛtʁ asi/ to be sitting. Both are in the AI section, with the seats that are not chairs: *un fauteuil* /œ̃ fotœj/, *un tabouret* /œ̃ tabuʁɛ/, *un banc* /œ̃ bɑ̃/.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **S'asseoir, to sit down.** A reflexive verb with two accepted sets of forms. The dictionaries' first set: *je m'assieds* /ʒə masje/, *tu t'assieds* /ty tasje/, *il s'assied* /il sasje/, *nous nous asseyons* /nu nuz‿asejɔ̃/, *vous vous asseyez* /vu vuz‿aseje/, *ils s'asseyent* /il sasɛj/. The second, heard as often: *je m'assois* /ʒə maswa/, *il s'assoit* /il saswa/, *nous nous assoyons* /nu nuz‿aswajɔ̃/, *ils s'assoient* /il saswa/. The past participle is *assis, assise* /asi, asiz/, with *être* as its auxiliary like every reflexive verb: *elle s'est assise* /ɛl sɛ(t)‿asiz/ she sat down. The imperative is the form you hear most: *assieds-toi* /asjetwa/, *asseyez-vous* /asejevu/.
- **Sitting, the state.** *Je m'assieds* is the action of sitting down; *je suis assis* /ʒə sɥi(z)‿asi/ is the state of sitting, *être* with the participle, which agrees: *elle est assise* /ɛl ɛ(t)‿asiz/ she is sitting. English *sit* covers both; French keeps them apart, and *Il est assis sur une chaise* /il ɛ(t)‿asi syʁ yn ʃɛz/ is the sentence to remember.
- **On, under, beside: the prepositions of place.** *sur* /syʁ/ on, *sous* /su/ under, *devant* /dəvɑ̃/ in front of, *derrière* /dɛʁjɛʁ/ behind, *à côté de* /a kote də/ beside, *entre* /ɑ̃tʁ/ between, *autour de* /otuʁ də/ around. One sits *sur une chaise* /syʁ yn ʃɛz/ but *dans un fauteuil* /dɑ̃z‿œ̃ fotœj/, on a chair and in an armchair, as in English. *Sur* has a page of its own, [sur](../sur/README.md).
- **The seats.** *Un siège* /œ̃ sjɛʒ/ is the general word, and the seat in a car or a theatre; *une place* /yn plas/ is a seat as a place to sit, *Cette place est prise ?* /sɛt plas ɛ pʁiz/ Is this seat taken? The parts of a chair are *le dossier* /lə dosje/ the back, *le siège* the seat and *les pieds* /le pje/ the legs, which are feet in French.
- **Chaise and chaire.** *La chaire* /la ʃɛʁ/, the pulpit and a professor's chair, is the same Latin word, *cathedra*, which also gave *la cathédrale* /la katedʁal/, the church with the bishop's chair. *La chair* /la ʃɛʁ/ flesh and *cher* /ʃɛʁ/ dear sound the same as *chaire*; none of the three sounds like *chaise*, whose last sound is /z/.
