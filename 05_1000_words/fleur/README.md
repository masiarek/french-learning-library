# une fleur — a flower

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *une fleur* /yn flœʁ/: *eu* before the French *r* is the open /œ/ of *la sœur* /la sœʁ/, *la peur* /la pœʁ/ the fear, *l'heure* /lœʁ/ the hour and *le beurre* /lə bœʁ/ the butter, and the *r* is said at the end. It is not the closed /ø/ of *deux* /dø/ and *peu* /pø/, where nothing follows the vowel: the same rule of position as /ɛ/ against /e/ on [Le son ɛ](../../04_Pronunciation/son_e_ouvert/README.md).
- *Une* /yn/ says the noun is feminine: *la fleur* /la flœʁ/, *les fleurs* /le flœʁ/, *des fleurs* /de flœʁ/, where the plural *-s* is silent and only the article changes in sound. Nouns in *-eur* are feminine when they name a quality or a feeling, *la couleur* /la kulœʁ/ the colour, *la peur* /la pœʁ/, *la douceur* /la dusœʁ/ the softness, and masculine when they name the one who does something, *un professeur* /œ̃ pʁɔfɛsœʁ/, *un vendeur* /œ̃ vɑ̃dœʁ/ a salesman; *le cœur* /lə kœʁ/ the heart and *le bonheur* /lə bɔnœʁ/ happiness are masculine too.
- The teacher's first lesson, [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md), has the flowers in the garden: *Dans le jardin, il y a des fleurs* /dɑ̃ lə ʒaʁdɛ̃, il j a de flœʁ/. Szypowska's [lesson 6](../../03_Szypowska/lecon_06_en_visite/README.md) has them as a kind in general, *J'aime les arbres et les fleurs* /ʒɛm lez‿aʁbʁ e le flœʁ/, with three flowers by name: *le lilas* /lə lila/ the lilac, *le muguet* /lə myɡɛ/ the lily of the valley and *l'œillet* /lœjɛ/ the carnation.
- The word family: *fleurir* /flœʁiʁ/ to flower, *fleuri, fleurie* /flœʁi/ in bloom, *un fleuriste, une fleuriste* /œ̃ flœʁist, yn flœʁist/ a florist, *la floraison* /la flɔʁɛzɔ̃/ the flowering, where the Latin *flor-* shows through with /ɔ/.
- The verb that goes with flowers is *offrir* /ɔfʁiʁ/ to give as a present: *offrir des fleurs à sa mère* /ɔfʁiʁ de flœʁ a sa mɛʁ/, the thing directly and the person with *à* /a/, as with *donner* /dɔne/ in Szypowska's lesson 6; and the thing one gives is *un bouquet de fleurs* /œ̃ bukɛ də flœʁ/.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **Offrir.** An *-ir* verb that conjugates like an *-er* verb in the present, as *ouvrir* /uvʁiʁ/ does on the page of [une fenêtre](../fenetre/README.md): *j'offre* /ʒɔfʁ/, *tu offres* /ty ɔfʁ/, *il offre* /il ɔfʁ/, *nous offrons* /nuz‿ɔfʁɔ̃/, *vous offrez* /vuz‿ɔfʁe/, *ils offrent* /ilz‿ɔfʁ/. The past participle is *offert* /ɔfɛʁ/: *il m'a offert des fleurs* /il ma ɔfɛʁ de flœʁ/ he gave me flowers. *Offrir* /ɔfʁiʁ/ takes the thing directly and the person with *à* /a/: *offrir des fleurs à Marie* /ɔfʁiʁ de flœʁ a maʁi/.
- **Fleurir.** A regular *-ir* verb of the *finir* /finiʁ/ family, with *-iss-* in the plural: *le cerisier fleurit* /lə səʁizje flœʁi/ the cherry tree is in flower, *les roses fleurissent en juin* /le ʁoz flœʁis ɑ̃ ʒɥɛ̃/. Its participle is an adjective: *un jardin fleuri* /œ̃ ʒaʁdɛ̃ flœʁi/, *une rue fleurie* /yn ʁy flœʁi/, where the *-e* changes nothing in sound. *En fleurs* /ɑ̃ flœʁ/ is *in bloom*: *les arbres sont en fleurs* /lez‿aʁbʁ sɔ̃t‿ɑ̃ flœʁ/.
- **Des fleurs, de belles fleurs, pas de fleurs, les fleurs.** The plural of *une fleur* /yn flœʁ/ is *des fleurs* /de flœʁ/; before an adjective that comes first, *des* /de/ shrinks to *de* /də/: *de belles fleurs* /də bɛl flœʁ/ beautiful flowers, *de jolies fleurs* /də ʒɔli flœʁ/. After a negative it is *pas de* /pa də/: *il n'y a pas de fleurs* /il nj a pa də flœʁ/, as on the page of [avoir](../avoir/README.md). And for the kind in general French wants the definite article where English has none: *j'aime les fleurs* /ʒɛm le flœʁ/ I like flowers.
- **The flowers.** *une rose* /yn ʁoz/, *une tulipe* /yn tylip/, *une marguerite* /yn maʁɡəʁit/ a daisy, *un tournesol* /œ̃ tuʁnəsɔl/ a sunflower, *une violette* /yn vjɔlɛt/, *un coquelicot* /œ̃ kɔkliko/ a poppy, *un lys* /œ̃ lis/ a lily, and the three of Szypowska's lesson, *le lilas, le muguet, l'œillet* /lə lila, lə myɡɛ, lœjɛ/. On the first of May the French give each other *le muguet du premier mai* /lə myɡɛ dy pʁəmje mɛ/ for luck. The parts: *un pétale* /œ̃ petal/, *une tige* /yn tiʒ/ a stem, and the flowers stand *dans un vase* /dɑ̃z‿œ̃ vɑz/ or grow *dans un pot de fleurs* /dɑ̃z‿œ̃ po də flœʁ/.
- **La fleur de l'âge.** *Être à la fleur de l'âge* /ɛtʁ a la flœʁ də lɑʒ/ is to be in the prime of life; *la fine fleur de* /la fin flœʁ də/ the cream of; *faire une fleur à quelqu'un* /fɛʁ yn flœʁ a kɛlkœ̃/ to do someone a favour; *arriver comme une fleur* /aʁive kɔm yn flœʁ/ to turn up fresh and unbothered; *avoir les nerfs à fleur de peau* /avwaʁ le nɛʁ a flœʁ də po/ to be on edge; *la fleur de sel* /la flœʁ də sɛl/ the salt skimmed from the surface; *la fleur de lys* /la flœʁ də lis/ the emblem of the French kings.
- **Fleur and pleure.** *La fleur* /la flœʁ/ and *il pleure* /il plœʁ/ he cries differ by their first consonant only, /f/ against /p/, and *le fleuve* /lə flœv/ a river that reaches the sea differs by the last. The rhymes in /œʁ/ are many: *la sœur, le cœur, la peur, l'heure, le beurre* /la sœʁ, lə kœʁ, la pœʁ, lœʁ, lə bœʁ/.
