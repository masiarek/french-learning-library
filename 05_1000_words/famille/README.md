# une famille — a family

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *une famille* /yn famij/: *-ille* at the end of a word is /ij/, the *y* of English *yes* after an /i/, with no /l/ at all, as in *la fille* /la fij/ the daughter, *gentille* /ʒɑ̃tij/ kind and *la vanille* /la vanij/. The exceptions keep the /l/: *la ville* /la vil/ the town, *mille* /mil/ a thousand, *tranquille* /tʁɑ̃kil/ calm, which Szypowska's [lesson 6](../../03_Szypowska/lecon_06_en_visite/README.md) has in *reste tranquille* /ʁɛst tʁɑ̃kil/, and *Lille* /lil/. The voice falls on the last syllable that is said, /fa-MIJ/.
- *Une* /yn/ says the noun is feminine: *la famille* /la famij/, *les familles* /le famij/, *ma famille* /ma famij/, *toute la famille* /tut la famij/ the whole family. *Famille* /famij/ is singular and takes a singular verb where English often says *my family are*: *ma famille est grande* /ma famij ɛ ɡʁɑ̃d/, *ma famille habite à Paris* /ma famij abit a paʁi/.
- The teacher's first lesson, [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md), opens with it: *J'habite dans une grande maison avec ma famille* /ʒabit dɑ̃z‿yn ɡʁɑ̃d mɛzɔ̃ avɛk ma famij/, and the answers have *avec sa famille* /avɛk sa famij/ with his family. *Grande* /ɡʁɑ̃d/ before *maison* /mɛzɔ̃/ is the feminine adjective with its /d/ said, as *française* /fʁɑ̃sɛz/ on the page of [une mère](../mere/README.md).
- The near family, *le père, la mère, les parents, le frère, la sœur, le fils, la fille* /lə pɛʁ, la mɛʁ, le paʁɑ̃, lə fʁɛʁ, la sœʁ, lə fis, la fij/, the grandparents and the in-laws, is on the page of [une mère](../mere/README.md). This page goes on to the other relatives, *l'oncle* /lɔ̃kl/, *la tante* /la tɑ̃t/, *le neveu* /lə nəvø/, *la nièce* /la njɛs/, *le cousin, la cousine* /lə kuzɛ̃, la kuzin/, *le mari, la femme* /lə maʁi, la fam/, and to the words made from *famille* /famij/: *familial* /familjal/, *familier* /familje/, *un nom de famille* /œ̃ nɔ̃ də famij/ a surname.
- *En famille* /ɑ̃ famij/ is *as a family, with the family*, with no article: *dîner en famille* /dine ɑ̃ famij/, *passer Noël en famille* /pase nɔɛl ɑ̃ famij/. *Une famille nombreuse* /yn famij nɔ̃bʁøz/ is a large family, which in France means three children or more, and *fonder une famille* /fɔ̃de yn famij/ to start one. A family's surname takes no plural *-s*: *la famille Lefèvre* /la famij ləfɛvʁ/ is *les Lefèvre* /le ləfɛvʁ/, the Lefèvres, as in Szypowska's [lesson 4](../../03_Szypowska/lecon_04_chez_les_lefevre/README.md), *Chez les Lefèvre* /ʃe le ləfɛvʁ/.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **The other members of the family.** *Les grands-parents* /le ɡʁɑ̃paʁɑ̃/; *l'oncle, la tante* /lɔ̃kl, la tɑ̃t/; *le neveu, la nièce* /lə nəvø, la njɛs/; *le cousin, la cousine* /lə kuzɛ̃, la kuzin/; *le mari, la femme* /lə maʁi, la fam/, where *la femme* /la fam/ is both the wife and the woman, and *mon mari* /mɔ̃ maʁi/, *ma femme* /ma fam/ are how spouses speak of each other; *le petit-fils, la petite-fille, les petits-enfants* /lə pətifis, la pətitfij, le pətiz‿ɑ̃fɑ̃/ the grandchildren; *le beau-frère, la belle-sœur* /lə bofʁɛʁ, la bɛlsœʁ/ the brother-in-law, the sister-in-law; *le gendre* /lə ʒɑ̃dʁ/ the son-in-law and *la belle-fille* /la bɛlfij/ the daughter-in-law, who is also the stepdaughter, as *belle-mère* /bɛlmɛʁ/ on the page of [une mère](../mere/README.md) is both the mother-in-law and the stepmother.
- **The eldest and the youngest.** *L'aîné, l'aînée* /lene/ the eldest, *le cadet, la cadette* /lə kadɛ, la kadɛt/ the younger one, *le benjamin, la benjamine* /lə bɛ̃ʒamɛ̃, la bɛ̃ʒamin/ the youngest of all; *les jumeaux, les jumelles* /le ʒymo, le ʒymɛl/ twins, boys and girls; *un enfant unique* /œ̃n‿ɑ̃fɑ̃ ynik/ an only child. *Un parent* /œ̃ paʁɑ̃/ is a parent and also a relative, *un parent éloigné* /œ̃ paʁɑ̃ elwaɲe/ a distant relative, and *les proches* /le pʁɔʃ/ are the close family.
- **The family of famille.** *familial, familiale* /familjal/ family as an adjective: *une réunion familiale* /yn ʁeynjɔ̃ familjal/ a family gathering, *les allocations familiales* /lez‿alɔkasjɔ̃ familjal/ the child benefit; *familier, familière* /familje, familjɛʁ/ familiar, and of a word, colloquial, the *fam.* of the dictionaries, *un mot familier* /œ̃ mo familje/; *se familiariser avec* /sə familjaʁize avɛk/ to get used to; *la parenté* /la paʁɑ̃te/ kinship.
- **Nom de famille, prénom.** *Un nom de famille* /œ̃ nɔ̃ də famij/ is the surname, *un prénom* /œ̃ pʁenɔ̃/ the first name, and the form asks for both, *nom et prénom* /nɔ̃ e pʁenɔ̃/. *Un air de famille* /œ̃n‿ɛʁ də famij/ is a family likeness, *un arbre généalogique* /œ̃n‿aʁbʁ ʒenealɔʒik/ a family tree, *une photo de famille* /yn fɔto də famij/ the photo of Szypowska's lesson 3, *un repas de famille* /œ̃ ʁəpa də famij/ a family meal, *la vie de famille* /la vi də famij/ family life.
- **C'est de famille.** *C'est de famille* /sɛ də famij/ it runs in the family; *tel père, tel fils* /tɛl pɛʁ, tɛl fis/ like father, like son; *une famille d'accueil* /yn famij dakœj/ a host family, and a foster family; *une famille recomposée* /yn famij ʁəkɔ̃poze/ a blended family; *une famille monoparentale* /yn famij mɔnopaʁɑ̃tal/ a single-parent family; *le chef de famille* /lə ʃɛf də famij/ the head of the family; *la famille royale* /la famij ʁwajal/; and the question for a friend, *Comment va la famille ?* /kɔmɑ̃ va la famij/ How is the family?
