The present of avoir, all six forms	j’ai, tu as, il a, nous avons, vous avez, ils ont /ʒe, ty a, il a, nuz‿avɔ̃, vuz‿ave, ilz‿ɔ̃/	grammar
ils ont or ils sont: what is the difference in sound?	The /z/ of the liaison: ils ont /ilz‿ɔ̃/ they have, ils sont /il sɔ̃/ they are	sound
How is eu, the participle, pronounced?	/y/, not /ø/: j’ai eu /ʒe y/ I had; the one common word where the letters eu are /y/	sound
The three irregular forms the app flags	ont /ɔ̃/ the ils present, ayez /ɛje/ the vous imperative and subjunctive, eu /y/ the past participle	grammar
Five things one has in French and is in English	avoir faim /fɛ̃/, soif /swaf/, froid /fʁwa/, peur /pœʁ/, vingt ans /vɛ̃t‿ɑ̃/: hungry, thirsty, cold, afraid, twenty	words
There is, there are, and three years ago	il y a /il j a/ for both; il y a trois ans /il j a tʁwaz‿ɑ̃/	grammar
J’ai un chat; and in the negative?	Je n’ai pas de chat /ʒə ne pa də ʃa/: after a negative avoir, un, une, des become de	grammar
a or à: which is the verb?	a without the accent is the verb (il a), à with the accent is the preposition to, at; both /a/	words
