# une maison — a house

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *une maison* /yn mɛzɔ̃/: *ai* is the open /ɛ/ of [Le son ɛ](../../04_Pronunciation/son_e_ouvert/README.md), the *s* between two vowels is /z/, and *-on* is the nasal /ɔ̃/ of [Les sons ɑ̃ et ɔ̃](../../04_Pronunciation/sons_an_on/README.md), where the *n* is not said as a consonant. Two syllables, and the word rhymes with *la raison* /la ʁɛzɔ̃/ the reason and *la saison* /la sɛzɔ̃/ the season.
- *Une* /yn/ says the noun is feminine: *la maison* /la mɛzɔ̃/, *ma maison* /ma mɛzɔ̃/, *les maisons* /le mɛzɔ̃/, *une grande maison* /yn ɡʁɑ̃d mɛzɔ̃/, *une belle maison* /yn bɛl mɛzɔ̃/, *une vieille maison* /yn vjɛj mɛzɔ̃/; the adjective takes its feminine, and the plural *-s* is silent.
- *Une maison* is a house, a building standing on its own with its door onto the street or the garden; a block of flats is *un immeuble* /œ̃n‿imœbl/, and a flat in it *un appartement* /œ̃n‿apaʁtəmɑ̃/. *Habiter dans une maison* /abite dɑ̃z‿yn mɛzɔ̃/, *habiter dans un appartement* /abite dɑ̃z‿œ̃n‿apaʁtəmɑ̃/: the teacher's true-or-false, *Adam habite dans un appartement* /adam abit dɑ̃z‿œ̃n‿apaʁtəmɑ̃/, is false because he lives in a house.
- *À la maison* /a la mɛzɔ̃/ is at home, whoever's home it is and whatever the building: *rester à la maison* /ʁɛste a la mɛzɔ̃/ to stay at home, *rentrer à la maison* /ʁɑ̃tʁe a la mɛzɔ̃/ to go home, *Je suis à la maison* /ʒə sɥi a la mɛzɔ̃/ I am at home. The other way to say it is *chez moi* /ʃe mwa/ at my place, *chez toi* /ʃe twa/, *chez nous* /ʃe nu/. English *home* has no one word in French; *la maison* and *chez* share the work.
- The word comes from the teacher's first lesson, [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md), which is named for it: *J'habite dans une grande maison avec ma famille* /ʒabit dɑ̃z‿yn ɡʁɑ̃d mɛzɔ̃ avɛk ma famij/, *Ma maison est belle et confortable* /ma mɛzɔ̃ ɛ bɛl e kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/, *Dans ma maison, il y a un salon, une cuisine, trois chambres et deux salles de bains* /dɑ̃ ma mɛzɔ̃, il j a œ̃ salɔ̃, yn kɥizin, tʁwa ʃɑ̃bʁ e dø sal də bɛ̃/; section 7 has *Il y a trois chambres dans la maison* /il j a tʁwa ʃɑ̃bʁ dɑ̃ la mɛzɔ̃/ and section 9 *La voiture est devant la maison* /la vwatyʁ ɛ dəvɑ̃ la mɛzɔ̃/.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **Habiter, to live.** A regular *-er* verb that begins with a vowel, so the small words before it elide and liaise: *j'habite* /ʒabit/, *tu habites* /ty abit/, *il habite* /il abit/, *nous habitons* /nuz‿abitɔ̃/, *vous habitez* /vuz‿abite/, *ils habitent* /ilz‿abit/; past participle *habité* /abite/. The *h* is mute, as in *l'hôtel* /lotɛl/. It takes *dans* for the building, *habiter dans une maison* /abite dɑ̃z‿yn mɛzɔ̃/, and *à* or *en* for the place, *habiter à Paris* /abite a paʁi/, *habiter en France* /abite ɑ̃ fʁɑ̃s/, *en Pologne* /ɑ̃ pɔlɔɲ/; *habiter une maison* /abite yn mɛzɔ̃/ without a preposition is said too. *Vivre* /vivʁ/ is to live in the wider sense, to be alive, to spend one's life; for an address it is *habiter*. *Où habites-tu ?* /u abit ty/ Where do you live? *J'habite rue du Bac* /ʒabit ʁy dy bak/, Szypowska's street.
- **À la maison and chez.** *À la maison* /a la mɛzɔ̃/ is at home or home, as a place: *rester à la maison* /ʁɛste a la mɛzɔ̃/, *rentrer à la maison* /ʁɑ̃tʁe a la mɛzɔ̃/, *travailler à la maison* /tʁavaje a la mɛzɔ̃/, *manger à la maison* /mɑ̃ʒe a la mɛzɔ̃/ rather than out. *Chez* /ʃe/ + a person is at that person's place: *chez moi* /ʃe mwa/, *chez mes parents* /ʃe me paʁɑ̃/, *chez le coiffeur* /ʃe lə kwafœʁ/ at the hairdresser's. *Fait maison* /fɛ mɛzɔ̃/ is home-made, on a menu, *une tarte maison* /yn taʁt mɛzɔ̃/, and the word does not agree there: *des gâteaux maison* /de ɡɑto mɛzɔ̃/.
- **House, flat, block.** *Une maison* /yn mɛzɔ̃/ a house, *un immeuble* /œ̃n‿imœbl/ a block of flats or an office building, *un appartement* /œ̃n‿apaʁtəmɑ̃/ a flat, *un studio* /œ̃ stydjo/ a one-room flat, *une villa* /yn vila/ a detached house with a garden, *un pavillon* /œ̃ pavijɔ̃/ a small suburban house, *un chalet* /œ̃ ʃalɛ/ a chalet in the mountains, *une ferme* /yn fɛʁm/ a farmhouse, *une maison de campagne* /yn mɛzɔ̃ də kɑ̃paɲ/ a house in the country, a second home, *une maisonnette* /yn mɛzɔnɛt/ a little house. *Un HLM* /œ̃n‿aʃɛlɛm/ is a council flat, three letters said one by one.
- **The parts of the house.** *Le toit* /lə twa/ the roof, *les murs* /le myʁ/ the walls, *la porte d'entrée* /la pɔʁt dɑ̃tʁe/ the front door, *les fenêtres* /le fənɛtʁ/ the windows, *l'escalier* /lɛskalje/ the stairs, *le rez-de-chaussée* /lə ʁedʃose/ the ground floor, *le premier étage* /lə pʁəmjɛʁ‿etaʒ/ the first floor, *la cave* /la kav/ the cellar, *le grenier* /lə ɡʁənje/ the attic, *le garage* /lə ɡaʁaʒ/, *le jardin* /lə ʒaʁdɛ̃/, *la cour* /la kuʁ/ the yard, *le balcon* /lə balkɔ̃/, *la terrasse* /la tɛʁas/, and next door *les voisins* /le vwazɛ̃/ the neighbours.
- **The verbs of the house.** *Acheter une maison* /aʃte yn mɛzɔ̃/ to buy, *vendre* /vɑ̃dʁ/ to sell, *louer* /lwe/ to rent, which goes both ways, *construire* /kɔ̃stʁɥiʁ/ to build, *déménager* /demenaʒe/ to move house, *emménager* /ɑ̃menaʒe/ to move in, *nous déménageons* /nu demenaʒɔ̃/ with the *e* of *nous rangeons*; *le propriétaire* /lə pʁɔpʁijetɛʁ/ the owner, *le locataire* /lə lɔkatɛʁ/ the tenant, *le loyer* /lə lwaje/ the rent.
- **The maison that is a firm.** *Une maison d'édition* /yn mɛzɔ̃ dedisjɔ̃/ a publishing house, *une maison de retraite* /yn mɛzɔ̃ də ʁətʁɛt/ an old people's home, *la maison des jeunes* /la mɛzɔ̃ de ʒœn/ the youth centre, *la Maison Blanche* /la mɛzɔ̃ blɑ̃ʃ/ the White House, *une grande maison* /yn ɡʁɑ̃d mɛzɔ̃/ a great firm, of champagne or of fashion; *le maître de maison, la maîtresse de maison* /lə mɛtʁ də mɛzɔ̃, la mɛtʁɛs də mɛzɔ̃/ the host, the hostess; *une femme au foyer* /yn fam o fwaje/ a housewife, *le foyer* /lə fwaje/ being the hearth and the home.
