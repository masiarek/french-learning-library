The four forms of joli, with IPA	joli, jolie, jolis, jolies, all /ʒɔli/: four spellings, one sound; only the plural before a vowel is heard, de jolis enfants /də ʒɔliz‿ɑ̃fɑ̃/	grammar
Why is jolie said like joli but petite not like petit?	The feminine -e makes a final consonant sound; joli ends in a vowel, so there is nothing to wake: joli, jolie /ʒɔli/ against petit, petite /pəti, pətit/	sound
Joli or beau: a pretty girl, a handsome man, fine weather	une jolie fille /yn ʒɔli fij/, un bel homme /œ̃ bɛl ɔm/, il fait beau /il fɛ bo/: joli is for the small and charming, beau for men, weather and the grand	words
A tidy sum, a clever move, a fine mess (ironic)	une jolie somme /yn ʒɔli sɔm/, un joli coup /œ̃ ʒɔli ku/, c’est du joli ! /sɛ dy ʒɔli/	words
Pretty expensive, in everyday French	joliment cher /ʒɔlimɑ̃ ʃɛʁ/: joliment slides from prettily to pretty, as English pretty does	words
Read aloud, then check: joli, Julie, jauni	/ʒɔli/ pretty, /ʒyli/ the name, /ʒoni/ turned yellow: /ɔ/, /y/, /o/	sound
Pretty houses: why de and not des?	Before a plural adjective that precedes its noun, careful French writes de: de jolies maisons /də ʒɔli mɛzɔ̃/	grammar
