# joli — pretty

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *joli* /ʒɔli/: the *j* is /ʒ/, the *s* of English *measure*, never the /dʒ/ of English *jolly*, which is this very word, borrowed from Old French *jolif* in the Middle Ages, from memory; the *o* before a consonant that is said is the open /ɔ/; and the *i* is /i/. Two syllables, both short.
- Four spellings, one sound: *joli, jolie, jolis, jolies* /ʒɔli/. The feminine adds an *-e* after a vowel, so there is no consonant to wake: *un joli jardin* /œ̃ ʒɔli ʒaʁdɛ̃/, *une jolie maison* /yn ʒɔli mɛzɔ̃/ are said alike, where *petit, petite* /pəti, pətit/ and *grand, grande* /ɡʁɑ̃, ɡʁɑ̃d/ differ. The agreement is on paper only, unlike *tout, toute* /tu, tut/ on the page of [tout](../tout/README.md), which agrees for the sound. Only the plural before a vowel is heard: *de jolis enfants* /də ʒɔliz‿ɑ̃fɑ̃/, *de jolies images* /də ʒɔliz‿imaʒ/.
- *Joli* goes **before** the noun, with *grand, petit, beau* /ɡʁɑ̃, pəti, bo/: *une jolie chambre* /yn ʒɔli ʃɑ̃bʁ/, *une jolie robe* /yn ʒɔli ʁɔb/, *un joli village* /œ̃ ʒɔli vilaʒ/, *de jolies fleurs* /də ʒɔli flœʁ/ with *des* become *de*.
- Pretty, not beautiful: *joli* is smaller in scale than *beau* /bo/, charming rather than striking. A girl, a dress, a garden, a village, a voice, a name can be *joli*; a man is *beau*, never *joli*; the weather is *beau*, *il fait beau* /il fɛ bo/. And *joli* has a sly use in *une jolie somme* /yn ʒɔli sɔm/ a tidy sum, *un joli coup* /œ̃ ʒɔli ku/ a clever move, *c'est du joli !* /sɛ dy ʒɔli/ a fine mess!
- The teacher's first lesson ends Adam's description with it: *Ma chambre est petite mais jolie* /ma ʃɑ̃bʁ ɛ pətit mɛ ʒɔli/, in [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md).

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **Agreement on paper.** *Joli* /ʒɔli/ masculine singular, *jolie* /ʒɔli/ feminine singular, *jolis* /ʒɔli/ masculine plural, *jolies* /ʒɔli/ feminine plural. The written forms agree with the noun, as every adjective does, and the sound never changes except in the plural liaison, *de jolis enfants* /də ʒɔliz‿ɑ̃fɑ̃/. Adjectives ending in a vowel all behave so: *vrai, vraie* /vʁɛ/, *bleu, bleue* /blø/, *fatigué, fatiguée* /fatiɡe/.
- **Before the noun, and des becomes de.** *Une jolie maison* /yn ʒɔli mɛzɔ̃/, *un joli jardin* /œ̃ ʒɔli ʒaʁdɛ̃/; in the plural, careful French writes *de jolies maisons* /də ʒɔli mɛzɔ̃/, *de jolis jardins* /də ʒɔli ʒaʁdɛ̃/. Before a vowel *joli* has no liaison in the singular, having no final consonant: *un joli appartement* /œ̃ ʒɔli apaʁtəmɑ̃/; the plural *s* is said as /z/: *de jolis appartements* /də ʒɔliz‿apaʁtəmɑ̃/.
- **Joli or beau.** *Joli* is pretty, nice, charming, on a small scale; *beau* /bo/ is beautiful, handsome, fine, on a large one. *Une jolie fille* /yn ʒɔli fij/ a pretty girl, *une belle femme* /yn bɛl fam/ a beautiful woman; *un joli village* /œ̃ ʒɔli vilaʒ/, *une belle ville* /yn bɛl vil/; *une jolie robe* /yn ʒɔli ʁɔb/, *un beau manteau* /œ̃ bo mɑ̃to/. Of a man, *beau* only; *un joli garçon* /œ̃ ʒɔli ɡaʁsɔ̃/ is a pretty boy, said of a child or with a smile. Of the weather, *beau* only. *Mignon, mignonne* /miɲɔ̃, miɲɔn/ is cute, *charmant, charmante* /ʃaʁmɑ̃, ʃaʁmɑ̃t/ charming, *ravissant, ravissante* /ʁavisɑ̃, ʁavisɑ̃t/ lovely; the opposites are *laid, laide* /lɛ, lɛd/ and, in everyday speech, *moche* /mɔʃ/ and *vilain, vilaine* /vilɛ̃, vilɛn/.
- **Comparing.** *Plus joli que* /ply ʒɔli kə/, *moins joli que* /mwɛ̃ ʒɔli kə/, *aussi joli que* /osi ʒɔli kə/; *le plus joli, la plus jolie* /lə ply ʒɔli, la ply ʒɔli/, *la plus jolie chambre de la maison* /la ply ʒɔli ʃɑ̃bʁ də la mɛzɔ̃/. *Très joli* /tʁɛ ʒɔli/, *vraiment joli* /vʁɛmɑ̃ ʒɔli/, *joli comme un cœur* /ʒɔli kɔm œ̃ kœʁ/ as pretty as a picture.
- **The sly senses.** *Une jolie somme* /yn ʒɔli sɔm/ a tidy sum, *un joli succès* /œ̃ ʒɔli syksɛ/ a nice success, *un joli coup* /œ̃ ʒɔli ku/ a clever move or a nice shot; the irony of *c'est du joli !* /sɛ dy ʒɔli/ a fine mess, and *ce n'est pas joli joli* /sə nɛ pa ʒɔli ʒɔli/ it is not very nice, with the word doubled; *faire le joli cœur* /fɛʁ lə ʒɔli kœʁ/ to play the charmer. *Joliment* /ʒɔlimɑ̃/ is prettily and, in everyday speech, pretty as in *joliment cher* /ʒɔlimɑ̃ ʃɛʁ/ pretty expensive, the same slide as English *pretty*.
- **The family.** *Joliment* /ʒɔlimɑ̃/; *enjoliver* /ɑ̃ʒɔlive/ to embellish, a story as much as a room; *un enjoliveur* /œ̃n‿ɑ̃ʒɔlivœʁ/, the thing that embellishes a wheel, a hubcap; *la joliesse* /la ʒɔljɛs/ prettiness, a word of books.
