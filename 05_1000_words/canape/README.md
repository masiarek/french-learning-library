# un canapé — a sofa

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *un canapé* /œ̃ kanape/: three syllables, three open vowels, /a/, /a/, /e/, and the stress on the last, as always in French. The *é* with its accent is the closed /e/ of *le café* /lə kafe/, *le thé* /lə te/, *le bébé* /lə bebe/, and it is said, where the final *-e* of *une table* /yn tabl/ is not. *Un sofa* /œ̃ sɔfa/ exists in French too, but the everyday word is *canapé*.
- *Un* /œ̃/ says the noun is masculine: *le canapé* /lə kanape/, *les canapés* /le kanape/, *des canapés* /de kanape/, *un grand canapé* /œ̃ ɡʁɑ̃ kanape/. The plural *-s* is silent; only the article changes in sound.
- Two things under one word: the sofa of the living room, and the party snack, a small piece of bread with something on it, *des canapés au saumon* /de kanape o somɔ̃/. The snack is named after the sofa: the topping sits on its slice of bread as a person sits on a sofa.
- The word comes from the teacher's first lesson, [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md): *Dans le salon, il y a un canapé, une télévision, une table et quatre chaises* /dɑ̃ lə salɔ̃, il j a œ̃ kanape, yn televizjɔ̃, yn tabl e katʁ ʃɛz/, and section 7 uses it to show *il y a* /il j a/: *Il y a un canapé dans le salon* /il j a œ̃ kanape dɑ̃ lə salɔ̃/.
- One sits *sur le canapé* /syʁ lə kanape/, on the sofa, as *sur une chaise* /syʁ yn ʃɛz/, but *dans un fauteuil* /dɑ̃z‿œ̃ fotœj/, in an armchair: the page of [une chaise](../chaise/README.md) has the seats and *s'asseoir* /saswaʁ/.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **Canapé-lit.** *Un canapé-lit* /œ̃ kanapeli/ is a sofa bed, also *un canapé convertible* /œ̃ kanape kɔ̃vɛʁtibl/ or just *un convertible* /œ̃ kɔ̃vɛʁtibl/, and in speech *un clic-clac* /œ̃ klikklak/, after the sound it makes when it folds down. The plural is *des canapés-lits* /de kanapeli/: both nouns take the *-s*, neither in sound. *Dormir sur le canapé* /dɔʁmiʁ syʁ lə kanape/ to sleep on the sofa is what the guest does when there is no spare room, and what a husband does after a quarrel, as in English.
- **The verbs of the sofa.** *S'asseoir sur le canapé* /saswaʁ syʁ lə kanape/ to sit down on the sofa, *être assis sur le canapé* /ɛtʁ asi syʁ lə kanape/ to be sitting on it, *s'allonger sur le canapé* /salɔ̃ʒe syʁ lə kanape/ to lie down on it, *s'affaler sur le canapé* /safale syʁ lə kanape/ to flop down on it, *faire la sieste sur le canapé* /fɛʁ la sjɛst syʁ lə kanape/ to have a nap on it. *S'allonger* is a regular *-er* verb with the *nous* spelling of *ranger* /ʁɑ̃ʒe/: *nous nous allongeons* /nu nuz‿alɔ̃ʒɔ̃/ keeps the *e* so that the *g* stays soft.
- **Kinds of sofa.** *Un canapé deux places* /œ̃ kanape dø plas/, *trois places* /tʁwa plas/, a two-seater, a three-seater, counted by its places as a car is; *un canapé d'angle* /œ̃ kanape dɑ̃ɡl/ a corner sofa; *un canapé en cuir* /œ̃ kanape ɑ̃ kɥiʁ/ a leather sofa, *en tissu* /ɑ̃ tisy/ in fabric, *en velours* /ɑ̃ vəluʁ/ in velvet: the material comes after *en* /ɑ̃/, as *une table en bois* /yn tabl ɑ̃ bwa/ a wooden table. The older words: *un divan* /œ̃ divɑ̃/, *un sofa* /œ̃ sɔfa/, *une banquette* /yn bɑ̃kɛt/ a bench seat, *une méridienne* /yn meʁidjɛn/ a day bed.
- **On the sofa.** *Un coussin* /œ̃ kusɛ̃/ a cushion, *un plaid* /œ̃ plɛd/ a throw, *la télécommande* /la telekɔmɑ̃d/ the remote control, which lives between the cushions, and *le chat* /lə ʃa/ the cat, who thinks the sofa is his.
- **The snack.** *Un canapé* is also a small open sandwich served with drinks: *des canapés au saumon* /de kanape o somɔ̃/ salmon canapés, *au fromage* /o fʁɔmaʒ/ with cheese, *pour l'apéritif* /puʁ lapeʁitif/ for the aperitif, *l'apéro* /lapeʁo/ in speech. The topping is named with *à* + the article: *au* /o/, *à la* /a la/, *aux* /o/.
