# un jardin — a garden

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *un jardin* /œ̃ ʒaʁdɛ̃/: *j* is /ʒ/, the *s* of English *measure*; the *r* is the French /ʁ/ of the throat; and the final *-in* is the nasal /ɛ̃/ of [Les sons ɑ̃ et ɔ̃](../../04_Pronunciation/sons_an_on/README.md), with no /n/ said at all. So *jardin* /ʒaʁdɛ̃/ rhymes with *le matin* /lə matɛ̃/, *le vin* /lə vɛ̃/, *le pain* /lə pɛ̃/ and *le cousin* /lə kuzɛ̃/. The *n* comes back as a consonant, and the vowel loses its nasal sound, as soon as a vowel follows it: *jardiner* /ʒaʁdine/ to garden, *le jardinage* /lə ʒaʁdinaʒ/ gardening, *un jardinier* /œ̃ ʒaʁdinje/ a gardener, the same change as *cousin, cousine* /kuzɛ̃, kuzin/.
- *Un* /œ̃/ says the noun is masculine: *le jardin* /lə ʒaʁdɛ̃/, *les jardins* /le ʒaʁdɛ̃/, *des jardins* /de ʒaʁdɛ̃/, *mon jardin* /mɔ̃ ʒaʁdɛ̃/. The plural *-s* is silent, so only the article changes in sound. Nouns in *-in* are masculine: *le matin* /lə matɛ̃/, *le vin* /lə vɛ̃/, *le chemin* /lə ʃəmɛ̃/ the way, *le voisin* /lə vwazɛ̃/ the neighbour.
- Adam's house in the teacher's first lesson, [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md), has one: *Il y a aussi un petit jardin* /il j a osi œ̃ pəti ʒaʁdɛ̃/, and in the prepositions of place, *Le jardin est derrière la maison* /lə ʒaʁdɛ̃ ɛ dɛʁjɛʁ la mɛzɔ̃/. *Petit* /pəti/ keeps its silent *t* before a consonant: *un petit jardin* /œ̃ pəti ʒaʁdɛ̃/, *un grand jardin* /œ̃ ɡʁɑ̃ ʒaʁdɛ̃/, *un joli jardin* /œ̃ ʒɔli ʒaʁdɛ̃/.
- *Un jardin* /œ̃ ʒaʁdɛ̃/ is also a park: *un jardin public* /œ̃ ʒaʁdɛ̃ pyblik/, *le Jardin du Luxembourg* /lə ʒaʁdɛ̃ dy lyksɑ̃buʁ/ and *le Jardin des Plantes* /lə ʒaʁdɛ̃ de plɑ̃t/ in Paris. *Un parc* /œ̃ paʁk/ is the bigger word, and *un jardin d'enfants* /œ̃ ʒaʁdɛ̃ dɑ̃fɑ̃/ is a kindergarten, the German *Kindergarten* translated.
- The verbs of the garden: *jardiner* /ʒaʁdine/, *faire du jardinage* /fɛʁ dy ʒaʁdinaʒ/ to do some gardening, *arroser* /aʁoze/ to water, *planter* /plɑ̃te/ to plant, *cueillir* /kœjiʁ/ to pick. The flowers in it have a page of their own, [une fleur](../fleur/README.md).

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **The nasal that comes and goes.** At the end of *jardin* /ʒaʁdɛ̃/ the *n* is not a consonant, it is the sign that the vowel is nasal. Put a vowel after it and the vowel turns oral and the /n/ is said: *jardiner* /ʒaʁdine/, *je jardine* /ʒə ʒaʁdin/, *le jardinage* /lə ʒaʁdinaʒ/, *un jardinier, une jardinière* /œ̃ ʒaʁdinje, yn ʒaʁdinjɛʁ/. The same rule makes *cousin, cousine* /kuzɛ̃, kuzin/, *voisin, voisine* /vwazɛ̃, vwazin/ and *vient, viennent* /vjɛ̃, vjɛn/ on the page of [venir](../venir/README.md).
- **Dans le jardin, au jardin, du jardin.** *Dans le jardin* /dɑ̃ lə ʒaʁdɛ̃/ in the garden is the usual phrase; *au jardin* /o ʒaʁdɛ̃/, from *à* /a/ + *le* /lə/, is heard too, *aller au jardin* /ale o ʒaʁdɛ̃/ to go into the garden. *Du jardin* /dy ʒaʁdɛ̃/, from *de* /də/ + *le* /lə/, says *of the garden* or *from the garden*: *les légumes du jardin* /le leɡym dy ʒaʁdɛ̃/ the vegetables from the garden, *la porte du jardin* /la pɔʁt dy ʒaʁdɛ̃/ the garden gate, *au fond du jardin* /o fɔ̃ dy ʒaʁdɛ̃/ at the bottom of the garden. *Au* /o/ and *du* /dy/ are the contracted articles of Szypowska's [lesson 4](../../03_Szypowska/lecon_04_chez_les_lefevre/README.md).
- **Jardiner.** A regular *-er* verb: *je jardine* /ʒə ʒaʁdin/, *tu jardines* /ty ʒaʁdin/, *il jardine* /il ʒaʁdin/, *nous jardinons* /nu ʒaʁdinɔ̃/, *vous jardinez* /vu ʒaʁdine/, *ils jardinent* /il ʒaʁdin/; past participle *jardiné* /ʒaʁdine/. The other verbs of the garden are regular too, except *cueillir* /kœjiʁ/ to pick, which conjugates like an *-er* verb in the present, *je cueille* /ʒə kœj/, *nous cueillons* /nu kœjɔ̃/, and whose *ue* after *c* is just a spelling for /œ/.
- **In the garden.** *la pelouse* /la pəluz/ the lawn, *l'herbe* /lɛʁb/ the grass, *un arbre* /œ̃n‿aʁbʁ/ a tree, *une fleur* /yn flœʁ/ a flower, *une haie* /yn ɛ/ a hedge, whose *h* is aspirate, so no liaison and no elision, *la haie* /la ɛ/, *les haies* /le ɛ/; *une allée* /yn ale/ a path, *un banc* /œ̃ bɑ̃/ a bench, *un arrosoir* /œ̃n‿aʁozwaʁ/ a watering can, *les légumes* /le leɡym/ the vegetables, *un portail* /œ̃ pɔʁtaj/ a gate, *un mur* /œ̃ myʁ/ a wall, and the garden chair of the page of [une chaise](../chaise/README.md), *une chaise de jardin* /yn ʃɛz də ʒaʁdɛ̃/.
- **Cultiver son jardin.** *Il faut cultiver notre jardin* /il fo kyltive nɔtʁ ʒaʁdɛ̃/ is the last sentence of Voltaire's *Candide* /kɑ̃did/, and *cultiver son jardin* /kyltive sɔ̃ ʒaʁdɛ̃/ means to mind one's own small affairs rather than the world's. *Un jardin secret* /œ̃ ʒaʁdɛ̃ səkʁɛ/ is one's private world. In the theatre, *côté jardin* /kote ʒaʁdɛ̃/ is stage left and *côté cour* /kote kuʁ/ stage right, seen from the audience, from the old theatre of the Tuileries, which had the garden on one side and the courtyard on the other.
