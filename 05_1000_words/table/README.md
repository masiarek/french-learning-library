# une table — a table

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *une table* /yn tabl/: one syllable. The *a* is the open /a/ and the final *-e* is silent, so the word ends in the cluster /bl/, both consonants said, as in *confortable* /kɔ̃fɔʁtabl/, *le sable* /lə sabl/ the sand, *le diable* /lə djabl/ the devil. English *table* has two syllables and a long vowel; French *table* has one syllable and a short one, and does not rhyme with English *able*.
- *Une* /yn/ says the noun is feminine: *la table* /la tabl/, *les tables* /le tabl/, *des tables* /de tabl/, *une grande table* /yn ɡʁɑ̃d tabl/, *une petite table* /yn pətit tabl/, *une table ronde* /yn tabl ʁɔ̃d/. The plural *-s* is silent.
- The table of the meal takes *à* /a/ and no article: *À table !* /a tabl/ is the call to the meal, dinner is served; *se mettre à table* /sə mɛtʁ a tabl/ to sit down to eat, *être à table* /ɛtʁ a tabl/ to be eating, *sortir de table* /sɔʁtiʁ də tabl/ to leave the table. The table as a thing has its article back: *mettre la table* /mɛtʁ la tabl/ to lay the table, *débarrasser la table* /debaʁase la tabl/ to clear it, *sur la table* /syʁ la tabl/ on the table.
- The word comes from the teacher's first lesson, [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md): *une table et quatre chaises* /yn tabl e katʁ ʃɛz/ in the living room; in section 7, *Il y a une table dans la cuisine* /il j a yn tabl dɑ̃ la kɥizin/; in section 9, *Le sac est sous la table* /lə sak ɛ su la tabl/. The chairs have their page, [une chaise](../chaise/README.md), with the prepositions of place.
- The same word gave *le tableau* /lə tablo/, the picture and the blackboard, *la tablette* /la tablɛt/, the shelf, the bar of chocolate and the tablet computer, and *le tablier* /lə tablije/, the apron, worn at the table and in the kitchen.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **Mettre, to put, and mettre la table.** *Mettre* /mɛtʁ/ is irregular: *je mets, tu mets, il met* /ʒə mɛ, ty mɛ, il mɛ/, all alike in sound, *nous mettons* /nu mɛtɔ̃/, *vous mettez* /vu mɛte/, *ils mettent* /il mɛt/, where the *t* comes back; past participle *mis* /mi/, *j'ai mis la table* /ʒe mi la tabl/ I have laid the table. The imperative is what one hears at home: *Mets la table !* /mɛ la tabl/, *Mettez la table !* /mɛte la tabl/. *Se mettre à table* /sə mɛtʁ a tabl/ is reflexive, to sit down to the meal: *On se met à table ?* /ɔ̃ sə mɛ a tabl/ Shall we sit down? *Débarrasser* /debaʁase/ is regular: *je débarrasse la table* /ʒə debaʁas la tabl/.
- **À table, sur la table.** *À table* /a tabl/ without an article is the meal: *À table !* dinner is served, *passer à table* /pase a tabl/ to go and eat, *rester à table* /ʁɛste a tabl/, *les bonnes manières à table* /le bɔn manjɛʁ a tabl/ table manners, *une bonne table* /yn bɔn tabl/ a good restaurant. With the article the table is furniture: *sur la table* /syʁ la tabl/ on the table, *sous la table* /su la tabl/ under it, *autour de la table* /otuʁ də la tabl/ around it, *au bout de la table* /o bu də la tabl/ at the end of it.
- **Kinds of table.** *Une table de cuisine* /yn tabl də kɥizin/ a kitchen table, *une table à manger* /yn tabl a mɑ̃ʒe/ a dining table, *une table basse* /yn tabl bas/ a coffee table, *une table de nuit* /yn tabl də nɥi/ or *une table de chevet* /yn tabl də ʃəvɛ/ a bedside table, *une table de jardin* /yn tabl də ʒaʁdɛ̃/, *une table pliante* /yn tabl plijɑ̃t/ a folding table, *une table ronde* /yn tabl ʁɔ̃d/, *une table en bois* /yn tabl ɑ̃ bwa/ a wooden table, *en verre* /ɑ̃ vɛʁ/ a glass one. The material comes after *en* /ɑ̃/, the use after *de* /də/ or *à* /a/.
- **What is on the table.** *Une nappe* /yn nap/ a tablecloth, *une assiette* /yn asjɛt/ a plate, *un verre* /œ̃ vɛʁ/ a glass, *un couteau* /œ̃ kuto/ a knife, *une fourchette* /yn fuʁʃɛt/ a fork, *une cuillère* /yn kɥijɛʁ/ a spoon, *une serviette* /yn sɛʁvjɛt/ a napkin, *le sel et le poivre* /lə sɛl e lə pwavʁ/ the salt and pepper, *le pain* /lə pɛ̃/ the bread, *une bouteille d'eau* /yn butɛj do/ a bottle of water.
- **The table that is a list.** *La table des matières* /la tabl de matjɛʁ/ the table of contents of a book, *la table de multiplication* /la tabl də myltiplikasjɔ̃/ the multiplication table, *une table ronde* /yn tabl ʁɔ̃d/ a round-table discussion as well as a round table, *faire table rase* /fɛʁ tabl ʁaz/ to make a clean sweep, from the Latin *tabula rasa*, the wax tablet scraped clean. *Un tableau* /œ̃ tablo/ is a picture on the wall, the blackboard at school, *Au tableau !* /o tablo/ come up to the board!, and a table of figures.
