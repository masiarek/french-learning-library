# une pièce — a room, a piece, a coin

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *une pièce* /yn pjɛs/: one syllable. *ie* before a consonant is /jɛ/, the glide of *pied* /pje/ with the open *e* of *père* /pɛʁ/, which the grave accent announces, and *-ce* is /s/, as in *France* /fʁɑ̃s/. The plural adds a silent *s*: *une pièce, des pièces* /yn pjɛs, de pjɛs/, so *la pièce* /la pjɛs/ and *les pièces* /le pjɛs/ differ only in the article, as *le lit, les lits* /lə li, le li/ do on the page of [un lit](../lit/README.md). Beside it: *la nièce* /la njɛs/ the niece, *l'espèce* /lɛspɛs/ the kind, *la pierre* /la pjɛʁ/ the stone, *le piège* /lə pjɛʒ/ the trap, and *le pied* /lə pje/ the foot, whose *e* is closed because no consonant is said after it.
- *Une* /yn/ says the noun is feminine: *la pièce* /la pjɛs/, *cette pièce* /sɛt pjɛs/, *une grande pièce claire* /yn ɡʁɑ̃d pjɛs klɛʁ/ a big, bright room, *une petite pièce sombre* /yn pətit pjɛs sɔ̃bʁ/ a small, dark one.
- One French word, six English ones. In a house, a room: *les pièces de la maison* /le pjɛs də la mɛzɔ̃/ the rooms of the house, the first heading of the teacher's [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md). Otherwise a piece or a part, *une pièce détachée* /yn pjɛs detaʃe/ a spare part; a coin, *une pièce de monnaie* /yn pjɛs də mɔnɛ/; a play, *une pièce de théâtre* /yn pjɛs də teatʁ/; a document, *une pièce d'identité* /yn pjɛs didɑ̃tite/, *une pièce jointe* /yn pjɛs ʒwɛ̃t/ the attachment of an e-mail; and *each*, *deux euros pièce* /døz‿øʁo pjɛs/ two euros each. The word after *pièce* picks the sense; alone, in a sentence about a flat, it is the room.
- The room words. *La pièce* /la pjɛs/ is any room, counted or named: *une pièce de plus* /yn pjɛs də plys/ one more room. *La chambre* /la ʃɑ̃bʁ/ is the bedroom, and a hotel room. *La salle* /la sal/ is a large room with a job: *la salle de bains, la salle à manger, la salle de classe* /la sal də bɛ̃, la sal a mɑ̃ʒe, la sal də klas/. *Le séjour* /lə seʒuʁ/ is the living room on a floor plan, *le salon* /lə salɔ̃/ the same room with its sofa. And English *room* as space is *la place* /la plas/: *il n'y a pas de place* /il nj a pa də plas/ there is no room; *la place* is never a room of the house.
- Counting rooms. A French advertisement counts *les pièces principales* /le pjɛs pʁɛ̃sipal/, the rooms for living and sleeping: *un trois-pièces* /œ̃ tʁwa pjɛs/, written *T3* /te tʁwa/ or *F3* /ɛf tʁwa/, is a living room and two bedrooms; the kitchen, the bathroom, the toilet, the entrance hall and the corridor are *pièces de service* /pjɛs də sɛʁvis/ and *dégagements* /deɡaʒmɑ̃/ and are not counted. The AI section sorts the rooms of the house that way, and the Polish count works the same, *mieszkanie trzypokojowe* with the kitchen apart.
- The root is Gaulish *pettia*, a bit, taken into Latin; English *piece* is the same word, borrowed from Old French, beside Italian *pezzo* and Spanish *pieza*. From memory.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **Which pièce?** The complement after the noun picks the sense, and most are *de* plus a bare noun: *une pièce de monnaie* /yn pjɛs də mɔnɛ/ a coin, *une pièce de théâtre* /yn pjɛs də teatʁ/ a play, *une pièce de rechange* /yn pjɛs də ʁəʃɑ̃ʒ/ a spare part, *une pièce de tissu* /yn pjɛs də tisy/ a length of cloth, *une pièce de viande* /yn pjɛs də vjɑ̃d/ a cut of meat, *une pièce d'eau* /yn pjɛs do/ an ornamental pond, *une pièce d'identité* /yn pjɛs didɑ̃tite/ an identity document. Others take an adjective: *une pièce jointe* /yn pjɛs ʒwɛ̃t/ an attachment, *une pièce détachée* /yn pjɛs detaʃe/ a spare part, *une pièce montée* /yn pjɛs mɔ̃te/ the tiered cake of a wedding, *la pièce maîtresse* /la pjɛs mɛtʁɛs/ the centrepiece, *une pièce rare* /yn pjɛs ʁaʁ/ a rare piece, for a collector, *une pièce unique* /yn pjɛs ynik/ a one-off. In a shop or a workshop, *à la pièce* /a la pjɛs/ singly, by the piece: *vendu à la pièce* /vɑ̃dy a la pjɛs/, *payé à la pièce* /peje a la pjɛs/ paid by the piece; and after a price, *pièce* alone means each, *un euro pièce* /œ̃n‿øʁo pjɛs/.
- **In pieces, from nothing, of a piece.** *Mettre en pièces* /mɛtʁ ɑ̃ pjɛs/ to tear to pieces; *de toutes pièces* /də tut pjɛs/ out of nothing: *inventer une histoire de toutes pièces* /ɛ̃vɑ̃te yn‿istwaʁ də tut pjɛs/ to make a story up entirely; *tout d'une pièce* /tu dyn pjɛs/ all of a piece, said of a blunt, straightforward person; *un maillot une pièce, un deux-pièces* /œ̃ majo yn pjɛs, œ̃ dø pjɛs/ a one-piece swimsuit, a bikini; *un costume trois-pièces* /œ̃ kɔstym tʁwa pjɛs/ a three-piece suit; and *rapiécer* /ʁapjese/ to patch, the verb of the oldest sense, a patch sewn on a garment.
- **Counting the rooms of a flat.** The rule French advertisements follow comes from the housing code's definition, from memory: a dwelling has *des pièces principales* /de pjɛs pʁɛ̃sipal/, for living and sleeping, and *des pièces de service* /de pjɛs də sɛʁvis/, the kitchen, the bathroom, the toilet, the laundry, the storeroom, plus *les dégagements* /le deɡaʒmɑ̃/, the entrance and the corridors. Only the first kind is counted: *un studio* /œ̃ stydjo/ is one room with a kitchen corner, *un deux-pièces* /œ̃ dø pjɛs/ a living room and a bedroom, *un trois-pièces* /œ̃ tʁwa pjɛs/ a living room and two bedrooms, and so on. The labels are *T2, T3* /te dø, te tʁwa/, the older *F2, F3* /ɛf dø, ɛf tʁwa/ still seen, and *2P, 3P* /dø pe, tʁwa pe/ on some sites. Agents also expect a counted room to be a room of its own with a window, about nine square metres or more, *neuf mètres carrés* /nœf mɛtʁ kaʁe/; a kitchen opened onto the living room, *une cuisine ouverte* /yn kɥizin uvɛʁt/ or *américaine* /ameʁikɛn/, is part of the *séjour* /seʒuʁ/ and changes the count by nothing. So *les pièces de la maison* /le pjɛs də la mɛzɔ̃/ on a worksheet names every room, and *un trois-pièces* in an advertisement names three of them. The how-sure table of these claims is on the teacher's [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md).
- **Verbs of the room.** *Entrer dans la pièce* /ɑ̃tʁe dɑ̃ la pjɛs/ to come into the room, *quitter la pièce* /kite la pjɛs/ to leave it, *traverser la pièce* /tʁavɛʁse la pjɛs/ to cross it, *aérer la pièce* /aeʁe la pjɛs/ to air it, *chauffer la pièce* /ʃofe la pjɛs/ to heat it, *la pièce donne sur le jardin* /la pjɛs dɔn syʁ lə ʒaʁdɛ̃/ the room looks onto the garden, as [une fenêtre](../fenetre/README.md) does, *la pièce fait quinze mètres carrés* /la pjɛs fɛ kɛ̃z mɛtʁ kaʁe/ the room is fifteen square metres, *une pièce aveugle* /yn pjɛs avœɡl/ a room without a window.
