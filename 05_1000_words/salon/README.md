# le salon — the living room

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *le salon* /lə salɔ̃/: two syllables, the open /a/ and the nasal /ɔ̃/ of *-on*, the vowel of *bon* /bɔ̃/, *non* /nɔ̃/, *la maison* /la mɛzɔ̃/, drilled in [Les sons ɑ̃ et ɔ̃](../../04_Pronunciation/sons_an_on/README.md); the *n* is not said as a consonant, it only nasalises the vowel. *Les salons* /le salɔ̃/ changes the article alone.
- *Le* /lə/ says the noun is masculine: *un salon* /œ̃ salɔ̃/, *un grand salon* /œ̃ ɡʁɑ̃ salɔ̃/, *un petit salon* /œ̃ pəti salɔ̃/, *un joli salon* /œ̃ ʒɔli salɔ̃/. It is *la salle* /la sal/, the room, with a suffix on it: a room for receiving, the room of good company.
- Three things under one word: the living room of a house, *dans le salon, il y a un canapé* /dɑ̃ lə salɔ̃, il j a œ̃ kanape/; a shop where a service is sold, *un salon de coiffure* /œ̃ salɔ̃ də kwafyʁ/ a hairdresser's, *un salon de thé* /œ̃ salɔ̃ də te/ a tea room; and a trade fair or an exhibition, *le Salon du livre* /lə salɔ̃ dy livʁ/ the book fair, *le Salon de l'auto* /lə salɔ̃ də loto/ the motor show, with a capital when it is the name of one.
- The word comes from the teacher's first lesson, [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md), which lists the rooms, *le salon, la cuisine, la chambre, la salle de bains* /lə salɔ̃, la kɥizin, la ʃɑ̃bʁ, la sal də bɛ̃/, and uses it to show *il y a* /il j a/: *Il y a un canapé dans le salon* /il j a œ̃ kanape dɑ̃ lə salɔ̃/. Szypowska's [lesson 4](../../03_Szypowska/lecon_04_chez_les_lefevre/README.md) walks through the rooms of a flat.
- *Le salon* has two near-synonyms: *le séjour* /lə seʒuʁ/ or *la salle de séjour* /la sal də seʒuʁ/, the living room on an estate agent's plan, and *la salle à manger* /la sal a mɑ̃ʒe/, the dining room, which is another room when the house has one and the same room when it does not.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **Dans le salon, au salon.** The living room takes *dans* /dɑ̃/ for what is in it and *au salon* /o salɔ̃/ for going there or being there: *Les invités sont au salon* /lez‿ɛ̃vite sɔ̃t‿o salɔ̃/ the guests are in the living room, *Passons au salon* /pasɔ̃ o salɔ̃/ let us go through to the living room, said after dinner. *Au* is *à* + *le*, the contracted article of *au lit* /o li/ on the page of [un lit](../lit/README.md); *du salon* /dy salɔ̃/ is *de* + *le*, *le canapé du salon* /lə kanape dy salɔ̃/ the living-room sofa.
- **Salon, séjour, salle, pièce.** *Le salon* is the room one receives in; *le séjour* /lə seʒuʁ/, *la salle de séjour* /la sal də seʒuʁ/, is the same room named for living in it, from *séjourner* /seʒuʁne/ to stay, and it is the word on a plan or in an advertisement, *un trois-pièces avec séjour* /œ̃ tʁwa pjɛs avɛk seʒuʁ/. *La salle* /la sal/ on its own is a large or shared room, *la salle à manger* /la sal a mɑ̃ʒe/ the dining room, *la salle de bains* /la sal də bɛ̃/, *la salle de classe* /la sal də klas/; *la pièce* /la pjɛs/ is a room when rooms are counted, *une maison de cinq pièces* /yn mɛzɔ̃ də sɛ̃k pjɛs/; and *une chambre* /yn ʃɑ̃bʁ/ is only the bedroom.
- **The shop called salon.** *Un salon de coiffure* /œ̃ salɔ̃ də kwafyʁ/ a hairdresser's, where *le coiffeur, la coiffeuse* /lə kwafœʁ, la kwaføz/ works, though one says *aller chez le coiffeur* /ale ʃe lə kwafœʁ/ to go to the hairdresser's; *un salon de thé* /œ̃ salɔ̃ də te/ a tea room; *un salon de beauté* /œ̃ salɔ̃ də bote/ a beauty salon; *un salon de massage* /œ̃ salɔ̃ də masaʒ/. English borrowed the word twice, as *salon* and, by way of the American West, as *saloon*.
- **The fair called Salon.** *Le Salon du livre* /lə salɔ̃ dy livʁ/ the book fair, *le Salon de l'auto* /lə salɔ̃ də loto/ the motor show, *le Salon de l'agriculture* /lə salɔ̃ də laɡʁikyltyʁ/ the farm show held in Paris every winter, *un salon professionnel* /œ̃ salɔ̃ pʁɔfɛsjɔnɛl/ a trade fair. The sense goes back to the *Salon* of the painters, the yearly exhibition in a room of the Louvre, and before that to the literary *salons* held in the drawing rooms of Paris.
- **What is in the living room.** *Un canapé* /œ̃ kanape/, *un fauteuil* /œ̃ fotœj/, *une table basse* /yn tabl bas/ a coffee table, *une télévision* /yn televizjɔ̃/, *une bibliothèque* /yn biblijɔtɛk/ a bookcase, *une étagère* /yn etaʒɛʁ/ a shelf, *une lampe* /yn lɑ̃p/, *un tapis* /œ̃ tapi/, *des rideaux* /de ʁido/ curtains, *un tableau* /œ̃ tablo/ a picture, *une cheminée* /yn ʃəmine/ a fireplace, *des plantes* /de plɑ̃t/ plants. *Un salon de jardin* /œ̃ salɔ̃ də ʒaʁdɛ̃/ is a set of garden furniture, and in a shop *un salon* is the sofa with its armchairs sold as a set.
