# la lie — the dregs

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *la lie* /la li/: one syllable, /li/. The final *-e* is silent, as in *amie* /ami/, and the vowel is the /i/ of *lit* /li/, so the word is said exactly like *le lit* /lə li/ the bed, *il lit* /il li/ he reads and *il lie* /il li/ he ties. The lesson [Homophones: lit, lie, lis](../../01_Sounds/homophones/README.md) runs the four through a program; here the point is the article, which is all the ear gets: *la lie* /la li/ is feminine, *le lit* /lə li/ masculine.
- What it is: the dead yeast and sediment that settle at the bottom of a cask or a bottle after fermentation, the lees. The wine trade says *les lies* /le li/ in the plural, and *sur lie* /syʁ li/ on a label of Muscadet /myskadɛ/ means the wine stayed on its lees over the winter and was bottled off them in spring. In everyday speech the deposit in an old bottle is *le dépôt* /lə depo/; *le marc* /lə maʁ/ is the pressed grape skins, and the brandy made from them.
- The figurative sense is the one most people meet: the lowest part of something, *la lie de la société* /la li də la sɔsjete/ the dregs of society, *la lie du peuple* /la li dy pœpl/ the rabble, both hard words. *Boire le calice jusqu'à la lie* /bwaʁ lə kalis ʒyska la li/ to drink the cup to the dregs is the Gospel's cup, *le calice* /lə kalis/, and means to go through an ordeal or a humiliation to its end.
- A substance, so it takes the partitive: *de la lie* /də la li/ some sediment, *pas de lie* /pa də li/ no sediment, *un peu de lie* /œ̃ pø də li/ a little. The AI section has the rule with the wine words.
- The root is Latin *lia*, a Gaulish word, and nothing to do with *le lit* /lə li/ (Latin *lectus*), *lire* /liʁ/ (*legere*) or *lier* /lje/ (*ligare*), whose *je lie, il lie* /ʒə li, il li/ are the same four letters. *Lier* is conjugated below, since *la lie* and *il lie* can only be told apart by the article against the pronoun.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **Lie and lier.** *Lier* /lje/ to tie, to bind, to link is a regular *-er* verb: *je lie, tu lies, il lie, nous lions, vous liez, ils lient* /ʒə li, ty li, il li, nu ljɔ̃, vu lje, il li/, participle *lié* /lje/, imperative *lie !* /li/ and *liez !* /lje/. Four of the six present forms are /li/, so *la lie* /la li/ the noun and *il lie* /il li/ the verb differ only in the word before them. The family: *lié à* /lje a/ linked to, *se lier d'amitié avec* /sə lje damitje avɛk/ to strike up a friendship with, *lier une sauce* /lje yn sos/ to thicken a sauce, *un lien* /œ̃ ljɛ̃/ a link or a bond, and the link on a web page, *la liaison* /la ljɛzɔ̃/ the liaison, of sounds and of lovers, *délier* /delje/ to untie, *relier* /ʁəlje/ to connect and to bind a book, *un allié* /œ̃n‿alje/ an ally.
- **The partitive: de la lie.** Something you cannot count takes *du, de la, de l'* in the singular: *du vin* /dy vɛ̃/ some wine, *de la lie* /də la li/ some sediment, *de l'eau* /də lo/ some water, and *des* /de/ in the plural, *des bouteilles* /de butɛj/. After a negative all four become *de*: *il n'y a pas de lie* /il nj a pa də li/, *il n'y a plus de vin* /il nj a ply də vɛ̃/ there is no wine left. After a word of quantity, *de* again: *un peu de lie* /œ̃ pø də li/, *beaucoup de vin* /boku də vɛ̃/, *une bouteille de vin* /yn butɛj də vɛ̃/. *Sur lie* /syʁ li/ on the label has no article at all, as a set label.
- **The wine words.** *Le vin rouge, blanc, rosé* /lə vɛ̃ ʁuʒ, blɑ̃, ʁoze/; *la bouteille* /la butɛj/ the bottle, *le bouchon* /lə buʃɔ̃/ the cork, *le tire-bouchon* /lə tiʁbuʃɔ̃/ the corkscrew, *le fond de la bouteille* /lə fɔ̃ də la butɛj/ the bottom of the bottle; *le tonneau* /lə tɔno/ the cask, *le fût* /lə fy/ the barrel, *la cave* /la kav/ the cellar; *la vendange* /la vɑ̃dɑ̃ʒ/ the grape harvest, *la levure* /la ləvyʁ/ the yeast, *la fermentation* /la fɛʁmɑ̃tasjɔ̃/, *le dépôt* /lə depo/ the deposit, *décanter* /dekɑ̃te/ to decant, *tirer le vin* /tiʁe lə vɛ̃/ to draw the wine off the lees, whence the proverb *le vin est tiré, il faut le boire* /lə vɛ̃ ɛ tiʁe, il fo lə bwaʁ/, in for a penny, in for a pound.
- **To the end.** *Boire le calice jusqu'à la lie* /bwaʁ lə kalis ʒyska la li/ is the strong, biblical way to say it; the plain ones are *jusqu'au bout* /ʒysko bu/ to the end and *jusqu'à la dernière goutte* /ʒyska la dɛʁnjɛʁ ɡut/ to the last drop. *Jusqu'à* /ʒyska/ until, as far as, contracts with the article: *jusqu'au* /ʒysko/, *jusqu'aux* /ʒysko/.
