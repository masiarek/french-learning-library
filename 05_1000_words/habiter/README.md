# habiter — to live

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *habiter* /abite/: the *h* is mute, so the word begins with a vowel for every purpose: *je* elides to *j'habite* /ʒabit/, and the liaison is made, *nous habitons* /nuz‿abitɔ̃/, *vous habitez* /vuz‿abite/, *ils habitent* /ilz‿abit/. Szypowska's [lesson 5](../../03_Szypowska/lecon_05_dialogue/README.md) has the mute *h*; the other *h*, the one of *le haut* /lə o/ and *les haricots* /le aʁiko/, blocks both elision and liaison, and a dictionary marks it with an asterisk.
- A regular *-er* verb, like *parler* /paʁle/: *j'habite, tu habites, il habite, nous habitons, vous habitez, ils habitent* /ʒabit, ty abit, il abit, nuz‿abitɔ̃, vuz‿abite, ilz‿abit/. Four of the six forms sound alike, /abit/, which is why the pronoun is never dropped, as the lesson on [homophones](../../01_Sounds/homophones/README.md) explains.
- *Habiter* is to live somewhere, to have one's home there. The place follows with the preposition the place wants: *habiter à Varsovie* /abite a vaʁsɔvi/, *habiter en Pologne* /abite ɑ̃ pɔlɔɲ/, *habiter dans une maison* /abite dɑ̃z‿yn mɛzɔ̃/, *habiter chez ses parents* /abite ʃe se paʁɑ̃/ to live at one's parents', and also with no preposition at all, *habiter une grande maison* /abite yn ɡʁɑ̃d mɛzɔ̃/.
- To be alive, to spend one's life, is *vivre* /vivʁ/: *il vit à Paris* /il vi a paʁi/ can replace *il habite à Paris* /il abit a paʁi/, but *vivre seul* /vivʁ sœl/ to live alone and *vivre bien* /vivʁ bjɛ̃/ to live well have no *habiter*.
- It opens the model text of section 10 of the teacher's [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md): *J'habite dans une grande maison avec ma famille.* /ʒabit dɑ̃z‿yn ɡʁɑ̃d mɛzɔ̃ avɛk ma famij/.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **The present, and the other tenses.** *J'habite* /ʒabit/, *nous habitons* /nuz‿abitɔ̃/, *vous habitez* /vuz‿abite/, the rest /abit/. Past: *j'ai habité* /ʒe abite/ I lived, I have lived, with *avoir* as the auxiliary; and the imparfait for a home one used to have, *j'habitais à Cracovie* /ʒabitɛ a kʁakɔvi/ I used to live in Kraków. Future: *j'habiterai* /ʒabitʁe/ I will live. The question: *Où habites-tu ?* /u abit ty/ or, as everyone says it, *Tu habites où ?* /ty abit u/; *Vous habitez où ?* /vuz‿abite u/.
- **Habiter where: the prepositions.** A city takes *à*: *à Varsovie, à Paris* /a vaʁsɔvi, a paʁi/. A feminine country takes *en*, a masculine one *au*, a plural *aux*: *en Pologne* /ɑ̃ pɔlɔɲ/, *en France* /ɑ̃ fʁɑ̃s/, *au Portugal* /o pɔʁtyɡal/, *aux États-Unis* /oz‿etazyni/. A building or a kind of home takes *dans* /dɑ̃/: *dans une maison* /dɑ̃z‿yn mɛzɔ̃/, *dans un appartement* /dɑ̃z‿œ̃n‿apaʁtəmɑ̃/, and a floor *au*: *au premier étage* /o pʁəmjɛʁ‿etaʒ/. Someone's home takes *chez* /ʃe/: *chez mes parents* /ʃe me paʁɑ̃/, *chez moi* /ʃe mwa/ at my place. A street takes nothing in everyday speech: *j'habite rue de la Paix* /ʒabit ʁy də la pɛ/.
- **Habiter with no preposition.** The verb can take the home as a direct object: *elle habite une grande maison* /ɛl abit yn ɡʁɑ̃d mɛzɔ̃/, *ils habitent le centre-ville* /ilz‿abit lə sɑ̃tʁəvil/ they live in the town centre. Both *habiter à Paris* and *habiter Paris* are heard.
- **Habiter or vivre.** *Habiter* /abite/ is the address; *vivre* /vivʁ/ is life: *il vit seul* /il vi sœl/ he lives alone, *elle a vécu dix ans en France* /ɛl a veky diz‿ɑ̃ ɑ̃ fʁɑ̃s/ she lived in France for ten years, *vivre bien* /vivʁ bjɛ̃/ to live well. *Loger* /lɔʒe/ is to lodge, to stay somewhere for a while, *résider* /ʁezide/ the official word on a form, *demeurer* /dəmœʁe/ the old one of the notary.
- **The family of the word.** *Un habitant* /œ̃n‿abitɑ̃/ an inhabitant, *les habitants de Varsovie* /lez‿abitɑ̃ də vaʁsɔvi/; *une habitation* /yn abitasjɔ̃/ a dwelling; *habitable* /abitabl/ fit to live in, *inhabité* /inabite/ uninhabited; *l'habitat* /labita/ the housing, and the habitat of an animal. The neighbours are *les voisins* /le vwazɛ̃/, the address *l'adresse* /ladʁɛs/, and to move house *déménager* /demenaʒe/, to move in *emménager* /ɑ̃menaʒe/.
