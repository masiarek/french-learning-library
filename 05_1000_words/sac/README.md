# un sac — a bag

## The word

Conventions: on the [chapter page](../README.md).

- *un sac* /œ̃ sak/: one short open /a/ and a final *c* that is said, /k/, as in *le lac* /lə lak/ the lake, *le parc* /lə paʁk/, *sec* /sɛk/ dry and *avec* /avɛk/ with. A final *c* is said after an oral vowel, with *le tabac* /lə taba/ and *l'estomac* /lɛstɔma/ as the exceptions to learn, and silent after a nasal vowel: *blanc* /blɑ̃/ white, *un banc* /œ̃ bɑ̃/ a bench, *franc* /fʁɑ̃/.
- *Un* /œ̃/ says the noun is masculine: *le sac* /lə sak/, *les sacs* /le sak/, *des sacs* /de sak/, *mon sac* /mɔ̃ sak/. The plural *-s* is silent and the *c* stays said, so one sack and ten sound alike but for the article.
- The teacher's first lesson, [La maison](../../02_Yosser_teacher/lesson_01_la_maison/README.md), has it under the prepositions of place: *Le sac est sous la table* /lə sak ɛ su la tabl/, with *sous* /su/ under against *sur* /syʁ/ on, the pair of the page of [sur](../sur/README.md).
- The kinds of bag say what they are for with *à* or *de*: *un sac à main* /œ̃ sak a mɛ̃/ a handbag, *un sac à dos* /œ̃ sak a do/ a rucksack, *un sac de couchage* /œ̃ sak də kuʃaʒ/ a sleeping bag, *un sac de voyage* /œ̃ sak də vwajaʒ/ a travel bag; which of the two a kind takes has to be learned with it. *En* says the material: *un sac en cuir* /œ̃ sak ɑ̃ kɥiʁ/ a leather bag, *un sac en plastique* /œ̃ sak ɑ̃ plastik/, shortened every day to *un sac plastique* /œ̃ sak plastik/, and the same way *un sac poubelle* /œ̃ sak pubɛl/ a bin bag.
- Two set phrases to know: *vider son sac* /vide sɔ̃ sak/, to empty one's bag, is to get it all off one's chest; *l'affaire est dans le sac* /lafɛʁ ɛ dɑ̃ lə sak/ is *it's in the bag*, the one that English borrowed.

## AI section: grammar, phrases, examples, exercises

- **The c that is said.** French drops most final consonants, but a final *c* after an oral vowel is said: *sac, lac, parc, sec, avec, un bec* /œ̃ bɛk/ a beak, *un truc* /œ̃ tʁyk/ a thing, *public* /pyblik/, *un choc* /œ̃ ʃɔk/. After a nasal vowel it is silent: *blanc, franc, un banc, le tronc* /lə tʁɔ̃/ the trunk. The exceptions with a silent *c* after an oral vowel are few, *le tabac, l'estomac, le porc* /lə pɔʁ/ pork. So *un sac* /œ̃ sak/ and *sec* /sɛk/ make a minimal pair on the vowel alone, /a/ against /ɛ/: *un sac sec* /œ̃ sak sɛk/ a dry bag.
- **Sac, sachet, sacoche.** The small words of the family: *un sachet* /œ̃ saʃɛ/ a small bag, a sachet, *un sachet de thé* /œ̃ saʃɛ də te/ a teabag; *une sacoche* /yn sakɔʃ/ a satchel, the bag of the postman and the bicycle. The other bags: *un cartable* /œ̃ kaʁtabl/ a schoolbag, *une valise* /yn valiz/ a suitcase, *un panier* /œ̃ panje/ a basket, *un cabas* /œ̃ kaba/ a shopping bag, *une poche* /yn pɔʃ/ a pocket, and in the south-west of France a plastic bag too; *un porte-monnaie* /œ̃ pɔʁtmɔnɛ/ a purse and *un portefeuille* /œ̃ pɔʁtəfœj/ a wallet, which go inside the bag.
- **Les courses.** *Faire les courses* /fɛʁ le kuʁs/ is to do the shopping, and *un sac de courses* /œ̃ sak də kuʁs/ or *un sac à provisions* /œ̃ sak a pʁɔvizjɔ̃/ is the bag for it. At the till the question is *Vous voulez un sac ?* /vu vule œ̃ sak/ Do you want a bag?, and the answer of the careful shopper is *Non merci, j'ai mon sac* /nɔ̃ mɛʁsi, ʒe mɔ̃ sak/; *un sac réutilisable* /œ̃ sak ʁeytilizabl/ is the reusable one.
- **The verbs of the bag.** *porter un sac* /pɔʁte œ̃ sak/ to carry a bag, *prendre son sac* /pʁɑ̃dʁ sɔ̃ sak/ to take one's bag, *poser son sac* /poze sɔ̃ sak/ to put it down, *ouvrir, fermer son sac* /uvʁiʁ, fɛʁme sɔ̃ sak/, *mettre quelque chose dans son sac* /mɛtʁ kɛlkə ʃoz dɑ̃ sɔ̃ sak/, *fouiller dans son sac* /fuje dɑ̃ sɔ̃ sak/ to rummage in one's bag, *oublier son sac* /ublije sɔ̃ sak/ to forget it, *perdre son sac* /pɛʁdʁ sɔ̃ sak/ to lose it, *faire son sac* /fɛʁ sɔ̃ sak/ to pack one's bag.
- **Plus d'un tour dans son sac.** *Avoir plus d'un tour dans son sac* /avwaʁ plys dœ̃ tuʁ dɑ̃ sɔ̃ sak/ is to have more than one trick up one's sleeve; *prendre quelqu'un la main dans le sac* /pʁɑ̃dʁ kɛlkœ̃ la mɛ̃ dɑ̃ lə sak/ to catch someone red-handed, with the hand in the bag; *mettre dans le même sac* /mɛtʁ dɑ̃ lə mɛm sak/ to lump together; *un sac d'os* /œ̃ sak do/ a bag of bones, where the plural *os* is /o/ and the singular *un os* /œ̃n‿ɔs/; *un sac de nœuds* /œ̃ sak də nø/ a tangle, a mess.
